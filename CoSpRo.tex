\begin{abstract}
Self-supervised representations can inherit spurious object--context associations from unlabeled images. We introduce CoSpRo, a method that uses sparse, language-aligned concepts to discover relations for more robust representation learning. A frozen CLIP/SpLiCE teacher identifies image pairs that become more similar after selected concept subspaces are removed. Activation contrast, residual concept agreement and synthetic null controls filter these relations into a sparse weighted graph. The graph guides batch composition and a relational objective alongside ordinary SimCLR, without target or group annotations during pretraining. Only the student encoder is needed at inference. A four-seed Waterbirds study yields higher mean worst-group accuracy than SimCLR and budget-matched raw-CLIP graph supervision. Semantic-neighbour and direct-transfer controls help separate the role of projected relations from the information in the frozen representation, while exposing uncertainty in the gains and imperfect preservation of target semantics.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
An image can contain several predictive factors. In Waterbirds, bird category correlates with background in the training set. A representation that strongly encodes this association can fail on birds shown in an uncommon environment. Worst-group evaluation makes these failures visible even when aggregate accuracy appears acceptable \citep{sagawa2020groupdro}. Self-supervised pretraining inherits the correlations present in its unlabeled images. Standard view augmentation alone does not specify which context changes should preserve object identity.

Our goal is to learn a visual encoder whose downstream predictions remain useful when object--context associations change, using no target labels or group annotations during graph construction and representation learning. A frozen vision-language model supplies semantic structure learned from external data. SpLiCE exposes this structure as sparse non-negative coordinates associated with text concepts \citep{bhalla2024splice}. We hypothesize that a concept subspace can explain why two images appear dissimilar. Removing that subspace may reveal a useful relation for student training. This hypothesis requires empirical checks: concepts can also encode object identity and projection can introduce incorrect neighbours.

CoSpRo constructs a fixed teacher graph from these projected relations. Each candidate relation is evaluated using geometric gain, concept activation contrast and agreement in the remaining sparse coordinates. Synthetic nulls calibrate group selection. The graph determines batch composition and supplies soft relational targets on the student backbone. At deployment, only the trained encoder and downstream classifier are required.

Our contribution is a concept-projected relation-discovery method and a controlled study of its transfer to an independently trained encoder. The graph exposes which concept removal produced each retained relation. The experimental design separates this choice of neighbours from graph-aware sampling and generic CLIP supervision. The four-seed study tests student transfer alongside graph quality and direct prediction of frozen SpLiCE representations.

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Selecting examples and relations in contrastive learning.}
SimCLR contrasts two views of an image against other images in the batch \citep{chen2020simclr}, making sample selection part of the learning signal. Hard-negative sampling emphasizes examples that are difficult to distinguish from an anchor without requiring labels \citep{robinson2021hard}. Debiased contrastive learning corrects contamination by same-class negatives \citep{chuang2020debiased}; false-negative cancellation instead identifies likely semantic matches for elimination or attraction \citep{huynh2022false}. NNCLR explicitly uses nearest neighbours as cross-image positives \citep{dwibedi2021nnclr}. CoSpRo also changes which images meet in a batch, but retains within-image SimCLR positives and all other images in its negative denominator. A separate backbone loss transfers the graph relations. Its sampler-only controls therefore distinguish gains from co-occurrence alone from gains that require relational supervision.

\paragraph{Relational supervision.}
Relational Knowledge Distillation transfers pairwise and higher-order structure without matching teacher and student feature coordinates \citep{park2019rkd}. ReSSL trains a student to match sharpened similarity distributions from a momentum teacher using weak augmentation \citep{zheng2021ressl}. Thus relational distribution matching itself is not our novelty. CoSpRo constructs a fixed external teacher by removing concept subspaces, auditing the resulting relations and retaining sparse evidence-weighted edges. The raw-CLIP and semantic-SpLiCE graphs test this relation-discovery choice under matched graph budgets. Direct reconstruction transfer tests an alternative use of the same frozen concept representation.

\paragraph{Concept representations, discovery and editing.}
TCAV measures prediction sensitivity along directions learned from concept examples \citep{kim2018tcav}; ACE discovers visual concepts by clustering image segments in feature space \citep{ghorbani2019ace}. Concept Bottleneck Models route predictions through annotated attributes that can be edited \citep{koh2020cbm}. Post-hoc Concept Bottleneck Models add an editable concept layer to pretrained representations \citep{yuksekgonul2023pcb}; Label-Free Concept Bottleneck Models use language-derived concepts without manual concept annotations \citep{oikarinen2023lfcbm}. These methods clarify different roles for concepts: explanation, prediction and intervention. CLIP supplies a shared image--text space \citep{radford2021clip}, and SpLiCE expresses its embeddings as sparse non-negative combinations of named text directions \citep{bhalla2024splice}. CoSpRo uses these coordinates to specify which information to suppress while discovering relations. Unlike a deployed concept bottleneck, the resulting student need not predict concepts or retain the teacher. A named direction is interpretable as an intervention coordinate, but is not necessarily a pure background factor.

\paragraph{Spurious features and invariance in SSL.}
Contrastive learning can suppress harder features in favor of shortcut solutions; implicit feature modification counteracts this through feature-space perturbations of the contrastive comparison \citep{robinson2021shortcut}. Content--style identifiability results explain how augmentation can isolate invariant content under explicit assumptions about the data-generating process and style changes \citep{vonkugelgen2021content}. CoSpRo instead searches for relations in a frozen semantic space; its projections do not satisfy those assumptions by construction. Learning-speed-aware SSL (LA-SSL) emphasizes slowly learned examples through adaptive sampling \citep{zhu2023lassl}. Our sampling is based on fixed concept-conditioned relations rather than online learning speed. We include a preliminary matched-budget LA-SSL adaptation in Appendix~\ref{app:lassl} and do not infer a multi-seed ranking from it.

\paragraph{Robustness with other supervision regimes.}
Group DRO uses annotated groups \citep{sagawa2020groupdro}, while Just Train Twice upweights errors of an initial supervised classifier \citep{liu2021jtt}. CoBalT discovers concepts without labels and uses them to balance supervised training \citep{arefin2024cobalt}. DIAL uses sparse-autoencoder analysis to remove spurious directions from VLM representations \citep{yalavarthi2026dial}. CoSpRo shares the idea of a representation-space intervention, but uses it to construct pretraining relations for a separate encoder. Supervised group-robust and zero-shot VLM accuracies therefore do not provide matched numerical comparisons to our SSL students and fixed linear probe.

\section{Problem Setting}
Let $\mathcal{D}=\{x_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$ be unlabeled training images with stable sample identifiers. The learned representation is $f_\theta(x)$. A downstream classifier $w$ predicts target $y$ from frozen features. For evaluation groups $g=(y,a)$ defined by target and context $a$, we report empirical average accuracy and worst-group accuracy:

\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{Avg}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{1}\{w(f_\theta(x_i))=y_i\},\qquad
 \mathrm{WGA}=\min_g\frac{1}{n_g}\sum_{i:g_i=g}\mathbf{1}\{w(f_\theta(x_i))=y_i\}.
\end{equation}

Graph discovery and SSL use images and frozen representations. Labels and group annotations enter downstream probe fitting, balanced probe-subset construction and post-hoc analysis. The discovery cache rejects annotation fields. ``Label-free'' refers to the graph and SSL stages. It does not describe CLIP's external image--text pretraining or the supervised evaluation stage.

\section{Method}
CoSpRo has four stages: encode images as sparse named concepts; group related concept directions; discover image relations by removing those directions; and transfer the resulting graph to a SimCLR student. The frozen branch runs once on training images. It supplies relations, not labels or deployed features (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}).

\subsection{Stage 1: SpLiCE concept representation}
Raw embedding similarity does not reveal which semantic factors make two images similar or different. SpLiCE provides a sparse vector whose non-negative coordinates measure contributions of named text directions to an image embedding. These coordinates let us test whether removing particular semantic information reveals a useful relation, without first annotating which concepts are spurious.

Given an image $x_i$, frozen OpenCLIP produces a unit embedding $z_i$. We center and normalize it using the fixed SpLiCE mean $\mu$. With a row-normalized text dictionary $D\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times d}$, positive sparse regression gives
\begin{equation}
 u_i=\operatorname{norm}(z_i-\mu),\qquad
 c_i\in\arg\min_{c\geq0}\|u_i-D^\top c\|_2^2+\lambda_1\|c\|_1.
\end{equation}
All vectors are columns and $\operatorname{norm}(v)=v/\|v\|_2$. The output is an aligned cache of full embeddings $u_i$, sparse codes $c_i$ and dictionary directions. The next stage uses the codes and dictionary to define interventions; the full embeddings remain available for measuring image similarity.

\subsection{Stage 2: Concept grouping}
Related words may describe overlapping semantic information. Removing only one direction can leave that information in another, so we allow related coordinates to form a joint subspace. We first exclude concepts that occur too rarely or almost everywhere. Lexical families are joined, and additional links require both high text-direction similarity and high coactivation across training images. Connected components form flat groups; isolated concepts remain singleton groups. This is a semantic grouping rule, not a classification into object and background concepts.

For each group $G$, an SVD of its dictionary directions gives an orthonormal basis $Q_G$ for their span. Its image-level activation is $A_i^G=\sum_{k\in G}c_{ik}$. The output consists of these bases and activation vectors, which specify what to remove and how strongly the corresponding concepts differ between images. Exact thresholds and numerical tolerances are in Appendix~\ref{app:provenance}.

\subsection{Stage 3: Teacher-graph construction}
A directed edge $i\!\to\!j$ means that image $j$ became a candidate neighbour of image $i$ after a concept group was removed, with increased similarity, contrasting group activation and agreement in the remaining concepts. Its weight measures this evidence. It does not certify that the pair has the same target or differs only in background.

\paragraph{Reveal and filter candidate relations.}
For each group, project the full centered embeddings outside its span and search for nearest neighbours in that space:
\begin{equation}
 u_i^{-G}=\operatorname{norm}(u_i-Q_GQ_G^\top u_i),\qquad
 \Delta_{ij}^G=(u_i^{-G})^\top u_j^{-G}-u_i^\top u_j.
 \label{eq:projection}
\end{equation}
An increased similarity suggests that this group contributed to the original separation. We retain candidates with gain above a positive minimum and $|A_i^G-A_j^G|$ above a quantile of positive-gain candidates. To reduce indiscriminate attraction, residual sparse codes $c_i^{-G}$, obtained by deleting the group's coordinates, must also agree:
\begin{equation}
 R_{ij}^{-G}=\cos(c_i^{-G},c_j^{-G})\geq\tau_R,\qquad
 e_{ij}^G=\Delta_{ij}^G\left(\tfrac12+\tfrac12R_{ij}^{-G}\right).
\end{equation}
Thus large geometric gain receives stronger evidence when the remaining concepts match. The projection uses $u_i$, not the sparse reconstruction $D^\top c_i$.

\paragraph{Audit groups and consolidate edges.}
A group should reveal relations beyond those induced by generic projection or accidental activation patterns. Its score $S(G)$ combines accepted gain, anchor coverage, residual agreement and alignment between activation contrast and gain, with a hubness penalty (Appendix~\ref{app:graphmath}). Equal-rank random subspaces and shuffled activations provide two null families. Their pooled score quantile $T_G$ calibrates the group. Sufficiently covered groups with $S(G)>T_G$ pass; a fixed cap retains those with the largest score excess. Null calibration is an operational filter, not a causal test or false-discovery guarantee.

Passing groups contribute evidence scaled by their relative null excess:
\begin{equation}
 r_G=\min\!\left(1,\frac{\max(0,S(G)-T_G)}{\max(|S(G)|,\epsilon)}\right),\quad
 \tilde e_{ij}=\max_{G\text{ retained}}r_Ge_{ij}^G.
\end{equation}
Missing group edges have evidence zero. We keep the strongest outgoing edges and cap destination indegrees to limit hubs. The final graph stores normalized donor preferences and absolute anchor confidence:
\begin{equation}
 p_T(j\mid i)=\frac{\tilde e_{ij}}{\sum_k\tilde e_{ik}},\qquad
 q_i=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}\max_j\tilde e_{ij}.
\end{equation}
These quantities are computed after pruning. Unsupported anchors have zero relational contribution. The output is a fixed sparse graph with concept attribution for each edge.

\subsection{Stage 4: Graph-guided SimCLR training}
The graph serves two purposes: it increases opportunities for related images to meet, and it tells the student how to distribute similarity among them. The sampler visits every training image once per epoch, placing an available sampled teacher neighbour in the same batch when possible. Ordinary two-view SimCLR remains the base objective. Graph neighbours are not substituted for its positives: they remain other-image negatives in its contrastive denominator. A separate loss on the backbone encourages agreement with teacher relations, while the contrastive head learns instance discrimination.

For a batch $B$, average the two normalized backbone views and renormalize, $s_i=\operatorname{norm}[\operatorname{norm}(h_i^{(1)})+\operatorname{norm}(h_i^{(2)})]$. The student's distribution includes every other image in the batch:
\begin{equation}
 p_S^B(j\mid i)=\frac{\exp(s_i^\top s_j/\tau_S)}
 {\sum_{k\in B:k\ne i}\exp(s_i^\top s_k/\tau_S)}.
\end{equation}
Restrict $p_T$ to available batch donors and renormalize to obtain $p_T^B$. If $B_S$ contains anchors with at least one such donor, training minimizes
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L(t)=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{SimCLR}}+
 \lambda(t)\frac{1}{|B_S|}\sum_{i\in B_S}q_i
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_T^B(\cdot\mid i)\,\|\,p_S^B(\cdot\mid i)).
 \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
The relational term is zero for an empty $B_S$. Confidence scales each anchor's contribution without changing its relative donor preferences. The graph weight warms up after an initial SimCLR-only period. Appendix~\ref{app:graphmath} gives the implemented SimCLR normalization, which fixes the relative loss scale. After pretraining, we discard CLIP, SpLiCE, the graph and the projection head. Only the student backbone and downstream classifier are used for prediction.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\paragraph{Data and representations.}
Waterbirds contains 4,795 training images with group counts 3,498, 184, 56 and 1,057 in $(y,a)$ order 00, 01, 10 and 11. Validation contains 1,199 images with group counts 467, 466, 133 and 133. The held-out test set contains 5,794 images with group counts 2,255, 2,255, 642 and 642. The frozen teacher is OpenCLIP ViT-B/32 with the \textit{laion2b\_s34b\_b79k} checkpoint. The dictionary uses the complete cleaned Open Images V7 vocabulary and sparse regression weight 0.25. The corresponding signal artifact has 20,931 concept coordinates. The image mean is the frozen SpLiCE centering vector stored in the cache.

\paragraph{Student optimization.}
The student is a randomly initialized ResNet-18 with the ImageNet-style stem (\textit{resnet18\_large}). Training uses 500 SSL epochs, batch size 128 and SGD with learning rate 0.01, momentum 0.9 and weight decay $10^{-4}$. Learning rate is multiplied by 0.1 at epochs 350, 400 and 450. SimCLR temperature is 0.05; relational temperature is 0.25. Standard views use random resized crops with minimum scale 0.2, horizontal flips, color jitter and grayscale conversion. The main study has student seeds 1--4 with one locked graph seed 0 and uses $\lambda_{\max}=0.5$ for both KL arms.

\paragraph{Linear evaluation.}
Every 25 SSL epochs, a frozen-feature logistic probe is fitted on the \textit{ds\_train} subset containing 56 examples per group (224 total). The subset uses training-group annotations. Training statistics standardize features. Full-batch float64 L-BFGS minimizes mean cross-entropy with L2 coefficient 0.001 on classifier weights. Convergence requires ten consecutive external optimization epochs with maximum absolute objective gradient below $10^{-6}$. Each external step allows up to 20 inner iterations with a safety limit of 200 external epochs. Reported metrics average the final ten converged probe optimization epochs. All student comparisons use the representation at SSL epoch 500. These ten optimization epochs are not independent experimental replicates. Standard deviations below are sample deviations across student seeds.

\paragraph{Controls.}
SimCLR uses standard batches and no graph loss. CoSpRo sampler-only uses graph-aware batches with KL weight zero. Raw-CLIP sampler-only provides the corresponding control for the raw teacher. The raw-CLIP teacher uses centered-CLIP neighbours with CoSpRo's supported anchors, outgoing row budgets, row weight profiles and anchor confidence. Both graphs obey the same maximum indegree. Each teacher induces its own batches, so the KL comparison must also be interpreted alongside the two sampler-only arms.

\paragraph{Reproducibility and held-out evaluation.}
Matched seeds share initialization; teacher loading and periodic probes are isolated from the training RNG. The historical evidence archive contains configurations, graph identities and all 680 periodic probe records from 34 graph-study runs. Updated control endpoints are recorded separately with run configurations and converged epoch-500 metrics. Every epoch-500 probe satisfies the convergence criterion. Historical probe views depend partly on the run seed, so the reported deviation describes the complete training-and-evaluation run. Some older saved feature tensors require reconstruction of the seeded loader order because they lack explicit sample IDs. The archived test-selection record identifies configurations and checkpoints but lacks a contemporaneous selection timestamp; it cannot independently verify when those choices were frozen. The reported evaluation uses the selected configuration, the same training subset for probe fitting and the epoch-500 encoder.

\subsection{Do projected relations add value beyond concept similarity?}
The semantic-graph control finds nearest neighbours of normalized SpLiCE reconstructions, preserving CoSpRo's supported anchors, row budgets, weight profiles, confidence and maximum indegree. Both use graph-aware batching and the same KL settings. Direct transfer instead predicts the normalized reconstruction through a separate head with cosine loss, ordinary batches and auxiliary weight 0.1 (Appendix~\ref{app:direct}). These comparisons distinguish projected relations, ordinary semantic relations and direct feature prediction; they are not pure loss ablations because neighbour identity changes batches and direct transfer uses a different head and weight.

\subsection{What do the retained relations capture?}
The graph has 12 selected concept groups and 12,239 directed edges, covering 4,582 of 4,795 training images (95.56\%), with maximum indegree 10. The selected concepts include Bamboo, Great grey owl and Tawny owl. All selected groups are singletons, so these experiments do not establish a benefit from multi-concept grouping. Selection discovers concept-dependent geometry rather than exclusively background concepts.

Post-hoc labels show both useful cross-context relations and target mismatches (Appendix~\ref{app:graphaudit}). For landbirds on water, same-target cross-background relations carry 59.22\% of CoSpRo's confidence-weighted graph mass versus 44.96\% for the raw graph. For waterbirds on land, the corresponding share barely changes (48.26\% versus 48.95\%), while wrong-target mass increases to 45.76\% from 39.73\%. These failures limit the interpretation of projection as nuisance removal even though the complete pipeline improves mean student WGA. Frozen-feature diagnostics confirm that the concept representation contains useful predictive information, but use different preprocessing and external pretraining; they are reported separately in Appendix~\ref{app:signal}.

\section{Limitations}
The experiments cover Waterbirds and one frozen graph seed. Four student seeds leave uncertainty about the average effect. The 224-example group-balanced probe uses labels and group annotations, so label-free pretraining does not make the full evaluation pipeline unsupervised. The results concern this probe regime and do not estimate performance under a large fully supervised training budget.

Concept names do not uniquely identify causal factors. A direction can contain both object and background information and subspace projection does not generate a counterfactual image. Null calibration compares operational scores; it does not establish causal identification or control a multiple-testing false-discovery rate. The wrong-target relations in the graph show why an increase in cosine similarity is not sufficient evidence of a useful training pair. Background remains linearly predictable from the student and the per-group results include losses as well as gains.

The method depends on the frozen encoder, its external pretraining data and the coverage of the text vocabulary. Sparse reconstruction also discards some CLIP information. Graph construction adds a frozen preprocessing cost, while each student uses the same 500-epoch budget as its controls. The teacher is not used at inference. The expanded direct-transfer and semantic-graph comparisons show substantial seed sensitivity. The latter supports only a tentative attribution of the gain to projection; neither establishes general superiority of relational transfer. The LA-SSL adaptation has one seed and does not support a reliable ranking against this external baseline.

\begin{samepage}
\section{Conclusion}
CoSpRo uses concept-subspace projections to discover relations for self-supervised pretraining. Its fixed teacher graph supplies a training signal to a separate encoder without target or group labels during discovery or SSL. The four-seed Waterbirds comparison yields higher mean average and worst-group accuracy than SimCLR and matched raw-CLIP supervision, providing positive empirical evidence of robustness benefits under this protocol. Four-seed validation controls favor projected relations on average, but semantic neighbours remain competitive and direct reconstruction transfer is seed-sensitive. These controls support a qualified empirical result rather than a general guarantee of nuisance removal or method superiority. The evidence motivates separating concept representation, relation quality and student transfer when designing robust self-supervised objectives.

\end{samepage}

\section{Graph and Loss Details}\label{app:graphmath}
The basis keeps singular values $\sigma>10^{-6}\max(\sigma_{\max},1)$. Projections with norm at most $10^{-12}$ are rejected; zero-norm residual codes receive similarity zero. The score is
\begin{equation}
 S(G)=\frac{\operatorname{medianGain}(G)\operatorname{coverage}(G)
 \operatorname{agreement}(G)\operatorname{alignment}(G)}
 {1+\operatorname{Gini}(G)+\operatorname{maxIndegree}(G)/N}.
\end{equation}
Gain, coverage, residual agreement and destination indegrees use accepted edges. Alignment is the correlation between activation difference and gain over positive-gain candidates before the residual gate, clipped to $[0,1]$ (zero for a degenerate correlation). Each random-subspace trial obtains an equal-rank basis from Gaussian directions, recomputes projected neighbours and retains the original group activation vector and residual coordinate mask. Each activation-shuffle trial retains the group's projection geometry and residual agreement while permuting activations across images. Both apply the same candidate-selection and scoring rules. There are 32 trials of each type; the pooled 0.95 score quantile gives $T_G$. Passing groups are ranked by $S(G)-T_G$, followed by strongest-evidence duplicate resolution, outgoing top-3 pruning and destination top-10 pruning. The reported graph has 504 candidate groups (503 singletons and one pair), 441 passing the null filter and 12 retained. These counts emphasize that the null audit is not a background detector or a multiple-testing correction.

For completeness, with normalized projection-head outputs $v_a$ for the $2|B|$ views, the implemented base objective is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{SimCLR}}=-\frac{\tau_C}{0.07}\frac{1}{2|B|}\sum_a
 \log\frac{\exp(v_a^\top v_{a^+}/\tau_C)}{\sum_{b\ne a}\exp(v_a^\top v_b/\tau_C)}.
\end{equation}
The positive $a^+$ is the other view of the same image. The prefactor $\tau_C/0.07$ sets its scale relative to Eq.~\ref{eq:objective}. The relational softmax masks the anchor's sample ID. KL starts after epoch 10 and warms over ten epochs to 0.5, then remains constant through epoch 500; this weight was selected in a preliminary development screen. Unsupported batches contribute zero graph loss. An empty-graph fallback exists in the trainer, but matched graph-control runs require a nonempty graph.

\section{Direct-Transfer Diagnostic}\label{app:direct}
We compare relational transfer with direct prediction of a frozen target. A separate head $g_\psi$ predicts a unit-normalized target $t_i$ from each student view:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{transfer}}=
 \frac{1}{2|B_V|}\sum_{i\in B_V}\sum_{v=1}^{2}
 \left[1-\cos\!\left(g_\psi(f_\theta(x_i^{(v)})),t_i\right)\right].
\end{equation}
Targets are centered raw-CLIP embeddings, normalized SpLiCE reconstructions or a fixed derangement of the reconstructions. $B_V$ contains valid target rows. The training objective is $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SimCLR}}+\alpha(t)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{transfer}}$; the matched SimCLR arm retains the head with $\alpha=0$. All four arms use ordinary batches, seeds 1 and 3, 500 epochs, SimCLR temperature 0.05 and maximum transfer weight 0.1 after the same delay and warm-up. \section{Preliminary LA-SSL Adaptation}\label{app:lassl}
We implement the learning-speed-aware sampling rule of \citet{zhu2023lassl} within the same ResNet-18, 500-epoch, batch-size-128 SimCLR and linear-probe protocol. Learning-speed estimates use exponentiated two-view projection similarity and an exponential moving average. The adaptation uses EMA coefficient 0.1, sampling scale 10 and quantile 0.1, updating every two epochs after a ten-epoch warm-up (first update at epoch 12). The coefficient is an explicit implementation choice; it is not specified in the cited algorithm. Sampling uses replacement with $N$ draws per epoch. Scoring requires an additional no-gradient pass, so equal training epochs do not imply equal computation. This is a protocol-matched adaptation, not a reproduction of the original paper's architecture or reported benchmark scores.

The completed seed 1 yields 50.38\% Avg. and 45.61\% WGA on validation, versus 50.29/44.54 for SimCLR and 55.80/51.61 for CoSpRo on the same seed. All probes converged at epoch 500. One seed, one adaptation setting and no held-out test result are insufficient to establish superiority over LA-SSL.

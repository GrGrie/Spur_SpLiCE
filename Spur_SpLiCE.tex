\title{SpLiCE-CRP: Concept-Projected Relational Pretraining for Label-Free Spurious Robustness}
\author{Anonymous Authors}

\begin{abstract}
Self-supervised representations can encode accidental correlations present in
unlabeled images. We introduce \emph{SpLiCE-CRP}, or Concept-Projected Relational
Pretraining, a label-free method that converts semantic concept interventions into
relational supervision for an independent SSL encoder. Frozen OpenCLIP and SpLiCE
provide language-readable concept directions and sparse image codes. A label-free
CoBalT discovery module finds recurring spatial concepts and produces
confidence-calibrated sample weights for balanced SpLiCE group formation. CRP
removes each group subspace from the full centered CLIP embedding and measures
which image relations become stronger. Residual SpLiCE agreement, reciprocal
projected neighbours and deterministic cross-fold persistence validate these
relations. Matched random subspaces and shuffled concept activations calibrate
selection. The resulting evidence forms a sparse fixed-density teacher graph. A
ResNet is trained from images with SimCLR and confidence-weighted relational
distillation on backbone similarities. Graph edges also supply weighted
contrastive positives. Target, spurious-attribute and group annotations are
reserved for downstream evaluation. Existing Waterbirds runs serve as single-seed
relational-mechanism pilots. Fresh end-to-end evaluation of the full pipeline and
multi-seed controls remain necessary.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Correlations remain present in images used for label-free pretraining. In
Waterbirds, bird type is correlated with background, so a contrastive encoder can
organize its representation around water and land context using image content
alone. A label-free robust method therefore needs alternative evidence about which
variations should exert less influence on representation geometry.

SpLiCE expresses a frozen CLIP embedding with sparse non-negative activations over
a language-aligned dictionary \citep{bhalla2024splice}. This creates an intervention
interface: a word group defines a semantic subspace and projecting it out asks
which image relations become visible when that factor has less influence. CoBalT
adds an object-centric view of the unlabeled training distribution. Its discovered
memberships balance the statistics used to form SpLiCE groups. A relation enters
the teacher graph when it is reciprocal, preserves the remaining readable concepts
and recurs across deterministic data folds.

SpLiCE-CRP has three stages. First, label-free CoBalT discovery produces fixed
spatial concept memberships and assignment confidence. Second, a frozen CRP audit
constructs a sparse teacher graph from balanced SpLiCE groups, full-space concept
interventions, residual concept agreement, cross-fold persistence and label-free
null controls. Third, a trainable ResNet receives this graph through a graph-aware
sampler, confidence-weighted relational KL and weighted positives in SimCLR. At
inference the ResNet backbone is the sole required component.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
 \item a language-readable, label-free procedure that proposes semantic factor
 subspaces and tests them by full-space intervention;
 \item an object-centric balancing stage that uses CoBalT memberships and spatial
 assignment confidence to calibrate SpLiCE group statistics;
 \item a sparse teacher graph whose edge and anchor confidence preserve the
 absolute strength of null-calibrated and cross-fold-validated frozen evidence;
 \item a combined SSL objective that transfers graph geometry into the backbone
 while preserving SimCLR and resolving graph-neighbour/negative conflict;
 \item a Waterbirds pilot with diagnostics that separate graph coverage, absolute
 confidence, unweighted KL and effective weighted KL.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Language-aligned concepts.}
TCAV introduced concept directions \citep{kim2018tcav}, ACE demonstrated automatic
concept discovery \citep{ghorbani2019ace} and Concept Bottleneck Models made
concepts explicit in prediction \citep{koh2020concept}. SpLiCE directly underlies
CRP because its sparse coordinates and text dictionary inhabit the CLIP space in
which we intervene \citep{bhalla2024splice}.

\paragraph{Spurious-correlation mitigation.}
Group DRO uses group annotations \citep{sagawa2020groupdro}; EIIL and Just Train
Twice retain target-dependent signals \citep{creager2021eiil,liu2021jtt}. CoBalT
learns object-centric concepts and uses them for supervised balancing
\citep{arefin2024cobalt}. We use its label-free discovery stage as a fixed upstream
source of confidence-calibrated balance weights. DIAL removes sparse directions
inside a vision-language model \citep{yalavarthi2026dial}. CRP uses frozen concept
removal to create relations for a separately trained image encoder.

\paragraph{Relational self-supervision.}
NNCLR shows that cross-image neighbours can supervise contrastive learning
\citep{dwibedi2021nnclr}. Correct-N-Contrast and Cross-Variant SSL construct robust
relations using stronger task information or predefined interventions
\citep{zhang2022cnc,yadav2026crossvariant}. Generic label-free methods such as
learning-speed-aware sampling and spectral regularization remain essential
same-setting baselines \citep{zhu2023lassl,ghanooni2025spectral}.

\section{Problem Setting}
Let $\mathcal{D}=\{(x_i,y_i,a_i)\}_{i=1}^{N}$, where $y_i$ is the downstream
target and $a_i$ a spurious attribute; evaluation groups are $g_i=(y_i,a_i)$.
During graph discovery and SSL training, CRP receives only images $x_i$ and stable
sample identifiers. Labels, attributes and groups are reserved for downstream
linear probing and explicitly post-hoc diagnostics.

After SSL training, a linear classifier is fitted on the frozen backbone. We report
average accuracy and worst-group accuracy (WGA). CRP uses a focused testable
inductive bias: projection of a relevant spurious factor should repeatedly reveal
relations that preserve the remaining readable concepts and avoid excessive hubs.

\section{SpLiCE-CRP}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Frozen cache and CoBalT-balanced semantic groups}
Frozen OpenCLIP produces normalized image embeddings $z_i$. With the SpLiCE image
mean $\mu$, CRP forms full centered embeddings
\begin{equation}u_i=\operatorname{norm}(z_i-\mu).\end{equation}
SpLiCE supplies non-negative sparse codes $c_i\in\mathbb{R}^{K}_{\geq0}$ and a
normalized text dictionary $D=[d_1^\top;\ldots;d_K^\top]$ such that
$u_i\approx D^\top c_i$. Cache rows are aligned by sample identifier and the cache
validator rejects hidden annotation fields.

CoBalT is trained first on two augmented views of each unlabeled image. A spatial
encoder maps each view to patch features. Learned slots compete for patches and
pool them into object-centric vectors. Cross-view attention distillation, slot
contrastive learning and vector quantization make these vectors stable across
crops. A momentum teacher assigns each active slot to a learned concept code. For
sample $i$, let $m_{ir}\in\{0,1\}$ indicate membership in discovered concept $r$.
The assignment confidence $\rho_i\in[0,1]$ is the spatial mean of the winning-slot
probability. CRP converts fixed memberships into mean-one sample weights
\begin{equation}
 n_r=\sum_i\rho_i m_{ir},\qquad
 \bar w_i=\rho_i\sum_r\frac{m_{ir}}{n_r},\qquad
 w_i=\frac{\bar w_i}{N^{-1}\sum_{\ell}\bar w_{\ell}}.
 \label{eq:cobalt-weights}
\end{equation}
Rare discovered concepts receive greater mass and uncertain assignments receive
less mass.

CRP filters SpLiCE concepts using the weighted unlabeled frequency
$f_k=N^{-1}\sum_iw_i\mathbf{1}[c_{ik}>0]$. Coactivation similarity is computed from
the weighted profiles $\sqrt{w_i}c_{ik}$. CRP joins trivial lexical variants and
joins other concepts when dictionary-direction similarity and weighted
coactivation pass fixed thresholds. These weights affect group proposal only. For
group $G$, SVD produces a stable orthonormal basis
\begin{equation}Q_G=\operatorname{orth}(D_G^\top).\end{equation}
Near-collinear directions are dropped, so the intervention depends on the subspace
geometry and remains invariant to repeated synonymous directions.

\subsection{Full-space concept intervention}
The canonical intervention removes the group span from the \emph{full} centered
CLIP vector:
\begin{equation}
 u_i^{-G}=\operatorname{norm}\!\left[u_i-(u_iQ_G)Q_G^\top\right].
 \label{eq:projection}
\end{equation}
The operation acts on the full centered embedding, so every component aligned with
$G$ is removed.

CRP finds top-$k_P$ projected neighbours. For candidate $j$, intervention gain is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_{ij}^{G}=\cos(u_i^{-G},u_j^{-G})-\cos(u_i,u_j).
 \label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
A pair is eligible when gain exceeds a minimum, summed group activations
$A_i^G=\sum_{k\in G}c_{ik}$ differ by at least a group-specific unlabeled quantile
and the projected relation is reciprocal. CRP also compares the remaining SpLiCE
codes after setting coordinates in $G$ to zero:
\begin{equation}
 R_{ij}^{-G}=\cos(c_i^{-G},c_j^{-G}).
 \label{eq:residual-agreement}
\end{equation}
Eligible pairs satisfy $R_{ij}^{-G}\geq\tau_R$. This gate asks whether the pair
still shares readable content outside the projected group.

Accepted-edge evidence is
\begin{equation}
 e_{ij}^{G}=\Delta_{ij}^{G}
 \left(\tfrac12+\tfrac12R_{ij}^{-G}\right).
 \label{eq:edge-confidence}
\end{equation}
For each group, CRP combines median accepted gain, anchor coverage, mean residual
agreement, non-negative activation-difference/gain correlation and hubness:
\begin{equation}
 S(G)=\frac{\operatorname{medianGain}(G)\operatorname{coverage}(G)
 \operatorname{residualAgreement}(G)\operatorname{alignment}(G)}
 {1+\operatorname{Gini}(G)+\operatorname{maxIndegree}(G)/N}.
 \label{eq:group-score}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Null calibration and teacher graph}
CRP compares $S(G)$ with equal-rank random-subspace scores and scores obtained after
shuffling group activations. Let $T_G$ be a fixed quantile of their combined null.
A group is retained when it passes minimum coverage and $S(G)>T_G$.

CRP then partitions the samples into deterministic folds. Within each fold it
recomputes projected neighbourhoods and accepted relations. The fold persistence
is the fraction of full-data accepted pairs among fold members that reappears after
this recomputation. Let $\pi_G$ denote mean persistence across folds. A retained
group satisfies $\pi_G\geq\tau_F$. Its pair evidence is scaled by relative null
margin and fold persistence:
\begin{equation}
 r_G=\min\!\left(1,\frac{\max(0,S(G)-T_G)}{\max(|S(G)|,\epsilon)}\right),
 \qquad \tilde e_{ij}^{G}=r_G\pi_Ge_{ij}^{G}.
 \label{eq:null-margin}
\end{equation}
An optional post-gate cap keeps groups with largest label-free null excess.

For duplicate directed pairs, CRP keeps the strongest group evidence. It retains
the three strongest outgoing edges per anchor and applies an absolute destination
indegree cap of ten.
Remaining confidences are row-normalized:
\begin{equation}
 p_T(j\mid i)=\frac{\tilde e_{ij}}
 {\sum_{k\in\mathcal{N}_T(i)}\tilde e_{ik}}.
 \label{eq:teacher}
\end{equation}
Relative teacher preference is separated from absolute anchor confidence,
\begin{equation}
 q_i=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}\max_j\tilde e_{ij}.
 \label{eq:absolute-confidence}
\end{equation}
This keeps weak evidence weak after row normalization. If no group passes, training
reduces to matched SimCLR.

\subsection{Graph-aware relational SSL}
A graph-aware batch sampler visits each image as an anchor once per epoch and when
possible, includes one neighbour sampled from $p_T(\cdot\mid i)$. It increases the
number of supported minibatch anchors while preserving the ordinary SSL stream.

For backbone features $h_i^{(1)},h_i^{(2)}$ of two views, CRP uses
\begin{equation}
 s_i=\operatorname{norm}\left[\operatorname{norm}(h_i^{(1)})+
 \operatorname{norm}(h_i^{(2)})\right].
\end{equation}
Within batch $B$, the student relation distribution is
\begin{equation}
 p_S^B(j\mid i)=\frac{\exp(\cos(s_i,s_j)/\tau_S)}
 {\sum_{k\in B:\,k\not\equiv i}\exp(\cos(s_i,s_k)/\tau_S)}.
 \label{eq:student}
\end{equation}
All occurrences of the same sample are masked. Restricting and renormalizing the
teacher to neighbours present in $B$ gives $p_T^B$. Supported anchors contribute
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rel}}=\frac{1}{|B_S|}\sum_{i\in B_S}q_i
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(p_T^B(\cdot\mid i)\,\|\,
 p_S^B(\cdot\mid i)\right).
 \label{eq:relational}
\end{equation}
The KL acts directly on backbone geometry; SimCLR uses its projection head.

Graph-linked samples become weighted additional NT-Xent positives with
symmetric weights derived from $q_i p_T(j\mid i)$. This avoids treating a teacher
neighbour as a SimCLR negative while KL attracts it. Denote the resulting loss by
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SimCLR}}^{+G}$. The complete implemented objective is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{L}(t)=\alpha\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SimCLR}}^{+G}
 +\lambda(t)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rel}}.
 \label{eq:total}
\end{equation}
After a pure-SimCLR start, $\lambda(t)$ ramps linearly to its configured value. A
late linear decay returns it to zero. Graph positives follow the same active
interval. Setting $\alpha=0$ defines a diagnostic KL-only ablation.

\section{Experimental Setup}
Waterbirds contains 4,795 training images and four bird/background groups
\citep{sagawa2020groupdro}. SSL sees only images and identifiers. Every 25 SSL
epochs, a 100-epoch linear probe is fitted on the frozen backbone and evaluated on
validation data. Reported Avg. and WGA are means over the final ten probes. A
separate background probe measures accessibility of the spurious attribute.

The student is ResNet-18-large with a 128-dimensional head, trained for 500 epochs
with batch 256, SGD learning rate 0.01, momentum 0.9, weight decay $10^{-4}$ and
SimCLR temperature 0.05. Learning rate drops by 0.1 at epochs 350, 400 and 450.
Resolved configuration, runtime versions, SSL diagnostics and evaluation metrics
are retained in W\&B.

The CoBalT discovery stage uses a from-scratch large-input ResNet-18 for 50 epochs,
four spatial slots, an eight-entry codebook and 32-dimensional slot vectors. The
main CRP audit uses CoBalT confidence in its balance weights, top-20 projected
neighbours, residual SpLiCE threshold 0.25, two deterministic folds, minimum fold
persistence 0.5, three outgoing graph edges and maximum indegree ten. It uses 32
trials for each null family and null quantile 0.95.

The completed loss-isolation runs disable graph positives and late decay, activate
KL after epoch 10, ramp for ten epochs and use $\tau_S=0.25$. They differ only in
$\lambda\in\{1,2\}$. These development pilots quantify the relational trainer and
fresh runs will evaluate the complete pipeline described above.

Increasing $\lambda$ from 1 to 2 improves Avg. by 1.70 points and WGA by 3.37
points. Relative to matched SimCLR, the stronger run improves Avg. by 5.21 points
and WGA by 9.88 points. Training remains numerically stable.

Diagnostics explain the effective scale. At the end of both runs, roughly 44\% of
minibatch anchors have teacher support and mean supported-anchor confidence is
about 0.00232. Confidence weighting reduces the unweighted KL of about 5.2 to about
0.012 before the scheduled multiplier. Thus $\lambda=2$ contributes about 0.024 to
the final loss. The best run's background probe remains high at about 91.9\%. The
supported statement is that CRP changes representation geometry and improves group
balance in this seed.

\section{Evidence Boundary and Limitations}
These results are mechanism-finding pilots. All runs use seed 0 and graph/loss
settings were developed with validation feedback. They measure the relational
trainer. The complete CoBalT-balanced pipeline awaits fresh evaluation. Paired
seeds are required before claiming stability. Required controls include raw-CLIP relational
distillation, random-subspace projection, shuffled SpLiCE codes, matched graph
density and a strong fully self-supervised baseline including spectral
regularization.

Core and context-related factors can remain entangled in task-agnostic unlabeled
data. SpLiCE concept subspaces can mix target and context information. Full
projection may therefore remove both. CoBalT memberships can emphasize recurring
objects that are unrelated to the desired robustness property. Residual SpLiCE
agreement shares the same readable concept system used to propose interventions.
Sparse groups can limit cross-context donors and dense candidate relations can
create false links or hubs. Finally, absolute confidence makes numerical loss
weights non-comparable across graph calibrations. Graph coverage, anchor
confidence, supported-batch fraction, unweighted KL, weighted KL and scheduled
weight must be reported together.

\section{Conclusion}
SpLiCE-CRP combines object-centric CoBalT balancing with language-aligned subspace
removal and transfers relations that survive residual-concept, reciprocal,
cross-fold, null and hubness checks. The fixed-density graph separates relative
teacher weights from absolute evidence confidence. A graph-aware sampler,
backbone-level KL and weighted SimCLR positives transfer its geometry into an
independent ResNet. The strongest completed relational pilot reaches 54.15\% Avg.
and 50.54\% WGA on Waterbirds. Fresh end-to-end runs, paired seeds and the full
control suite are required to evaluate the complete pipeline.

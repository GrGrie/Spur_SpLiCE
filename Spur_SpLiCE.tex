\begin{abstract}
Self-supervised learning (SSL) learns representations from unlabeled data. Its feature geometry can still follow accidental correlations such as the association between bird type and background in Waterbirds. Existing robust methods often receive target labels, group annotations or a predefined spurious attribute. We study a label-free pretraining setting in which the relevant shortcut factor is unknown.

We propose \emph{SpLiCE-CRP}, a framework for Concept-Projected Relational Pretraining. A frozen SpLiCE model decomposes CLIP image embeddings into sparse language-aligned concepts and groups related concepts into candidate semantic factors. For each group, CRP projects the full centered CLIP embedding away from the corresponding concept subspace and finds image pairs whose similarity increases after this counterfactual intervention. Reciprocal projected neighbours, independent DINO support and random-subspace and shuffled-activation controls filter candidate relations. Their confidence-weighted edges form a sparse teacher graph. A standalone ResNet is trained to match this graph while retaining its standard SimCLR objective. SpLiCE-CRP thereby converts interpretable concept interventions into label-free relational supervision using unlabeled images and frozen feature extractors during pretraining.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Self-supervised learning (SSL) removes class labels from pretraining. Shortcuts present in the data can still shape the learned representation. On Waterbirds, a SimCLR representation can support reasonable average classification while retaining a large gap between birds observed in common and uncommon backgrounds. Standard image augmentations provide implicit semantic interventions. Purely geometric regularizers provide an underspecified target for feature suppression.

SpLiCE decomposes CLIP embeddings into sparse non-negative coordinates over language-aligned text concepts \citep{bhalla2024splice}. This suggests a useful interface: a frozen concept model may identify factors such as vegetation, water, colour, texture or demographic appearance and a separate SSL encoder can learn an invariant representation. Interpretable words form the starting point. A label-free method must also identify the factor for intervention and transfer the resulting invariance into a new encoder.

SpLiCE-CRP uses a label-free confidence-weighted relation graph obtained through full-space concept projection and independent DINO agreement. Concept projection acts as a controlled experiment in frozen CLIP space: if removing a semantic factor reveals a stable visual relation, that relation becomes supervision for a separate SSL encoder. The final ResNet uses its own representation and requires only an image at inference time.

Our preliminary Waterbirds evaluation establishes that this training mechanism is effective across a meaningful region of its hyperparameter space. SpLiCE-CRP improves validation WGA in 10 of 13 tested configurations. The strongest single-seed configuration increases WGA by $8.28$ points and average accuracy by $4.55$ points over a matched SimCLR baseline. A frozen graph audit further characterizes which relations supply this gain and identifies relation diversity as a central direction for continued study.

Our current contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item SpLiCE-CRP, a label-free interface that converts interpretable concept interventions into a soft teacher graph for an independent SSL encoder;
    \item a relation-discovery procedure combining full-space concept projection, reciprocal neighbourhoods, independent DINO support and calibrated null controls;
    \item a controlled Waterbirds study showing improved average and worst-group validation accuracy together with a sensitivity analysis and post-hoc graph audit.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work and Positioning}
\paragraph{Concepts as an interpretable intervention interface.}
Concept-based XAI provides the interface through which SpLiCE-CRP formulates semantic interventions. TCAV established concept directions as a way to measure model sensitivity to human-defined factors \citep{kim2018tcav} and ACE showed that coherent concepts can be discovered automatically from visual data \citep{ghorbani2019ace}. Concept Bottleneck Models are a foundational example of actionable concept-based explanations: concepts appear explicitly in the predictive path and users can intervene on them \citep{koh2020concept}. Neural Concept Binder extends this principle to inspectable and revisable object--factor concepts learned from unlabeled images \citep{stammer2024ncb}. SpLiCE is our direct technical foundation because it expresses a dense CLIP image embedding through sparse non-negative activations over a language-aligned vocabulary \citep{bhalla2024splice}. We use these activations to identify semantic subspaces in a frozen teacher representation and transfer the relations revealed by their removal into a separate encoder.

\paragraph{Concept-guided mitigation of spurious correlations.}
This line of work shares our aim of identifying and weakening semantically meaningful shortcut factors. Group DRO provides the canonical supervised reference by optimizing worst-group risk from group annotations \citep{sagawa2020groupdro}. EIIL infers environments from a reference classifier \citep{creager2021eiil} and Just Train Twice identifies examples for reweighting from initial ERM errors \citep{liu2021jtt}; both reduce annotation requirements while retaining a target-dependent robust signal. CoBalT discovers object-centric concepts from unlabeled images and uses class labels to balance supervised training across the discovered structure \citep{arefin2024cobalt}. Bias Leaves a Gradient Trail ranks concept directions through target gradients and suppresses them in a frozen classifier \citep{vitry2026gradient}. DIAL is the closest work in objective: it discovers sparse-autoencoder directions in a vision-language model and removes spurious directions using only unlabeled data \citep{yalavarthi2026dial}. SpLiCE-CRP pursues the same label-free mitigation goal through a different transfer mechanism. Concept removal acts as a temporary experiment in frozen CLIP space and the deployed ResNet receives the resulting image relations.

\paragraph{Spurious-robust and relational self-supervision.}
The closest SSL baselines operate in the same label-free training regime and define robustness through general-purpose changes to sampling or geometry. Learning-speed-aware SSL adapts sampling from training dynamics \citep{zhu2023lassl}, feature-space augmentation expands the variation presented to the learner \citep{hamidieh2024views} and spectral regularization reduces concentration in the representation covariance spectrum \citep{ghanooni2025spectral}. These methods test whether an interpretable factor-specific intervention adds information beyond generic SSL regularization. Relational SSL provides the second foundation for our method. NNCLR demonstrates that cross-image neighbours can supervise an SSL encoder \citep{dwibedi2021nnclr}. Correct-N-Contrast constructs robust same-class relations from target labels and an ERM model \citep{zhang2022cnc}. Cross-Variant SSL creates background-randomized image pairs through a predefined pixel-space intervention \citep{yadav2026crossvariant}. SpLiCE-CRP instead discovers a soft relation graph through language-aligned subspace interventions and independent DINO agreement. This graph transfers counterfactual neighbourhood structure while the ResNet retains its own feature coordinates.

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{Problem Setting}
Let $\mathcal{D}=\{(x_i,y_i,a_i)\}_{i=1}^{N}$ denote images $x_i$, downstream targets $y_i$ and spurious attributes $a_i$. Each evaluation group is $g_i=(y_i,a_i)$. During SSL pretraining the learner receives only the images $\{x_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$. A trainable encoder $f_\theta$ processes two stochastic views of every image and a projection head is optimized with the SimCLR NT-Xent objective \citep{chen2020simclr}. After pretraining, a linear probe predicts $y$ from the frozen backbone. Average accuracy and worst-group accuracy (WGA), defined as the minimum accuracy over $g$, quantify downstream quality.

A spurious correlation can shape local feature geometry even when SSL uses no class labels. Waterbirds gives a concrete example: bird type and background are strongly associated in the training split. An encoder can therefore place two visually and semantically compatible birds far apart because one appears on water and the other in vegetation. The desired representation preserves information useful for $y$ and gives less geometric influence to such shortcut factors.

SpLiCE-CRP treats a semantic factor as a candidate shortcut when removing its subspace reveals stable image relations supported by an independent visual representation. This operational criterion supplies the inductive bias required by the label-free setting. Task relevance remains dataset-dependent and is measured after pretraining. Concept discovery, factor selection, graph construction and student training use unlabeled images, sample identifiers, frozen CLIP/SpLiCE features and frozen DINO features. Target labels $y_i$, attributes $a_i$, groups $g_i$ and WGA are reserved for linear probing, evaluation, post-hoc analysis and explicitly marked oracle experiments.

\subsection{SpLiCE-CRP}
\label{sec:crp}
SpLiCE-CRP follows one sequence from an image to an additional SSL signal. Frozen CLIP and SpLiCE propose an interpretable factor, projection asks how image relations change after removing that factor and frozen DINO checks whether the revealed relations remain visually plausible. The accepted relations form a soft teacher graph that supervises a standalone ResNet. The method transfers neighbourhood structure and leaves the student free to organize its own feature coordinates.

\paragraph{Describing images and constructing semantic factor subspaces.}
For every training image $x_i$, a frozen CLIP image encoder produces a dense embedding $z_i\in\mathbb{R}^{d_c}$. We center it with the training-set mean $\mu$ and normalize it:
\begin{equation}
    u_i=\operatorname{norm}(z_i-\mu).
\end{equation}
This vector contains the full CLIP description of the image. In Waterbirds, it may jointly encode the bird, water, vegetation, colour and texture. SpLiCE makes this description interpretable through a dictionary $D=[d_1^\top;\ldots;d_K^\top]$ of CLIP text directions:
\begin{equation}
    u_i\approx D^\top c_i=\sum_{k=1}^{K}c_{ik}d_k,
    \qquad c_i\geq0 \quad\text{and}\quad c_i\ \text{is sparse}.
    \label{eq:splice_decomposition}
\end{equation}
We use the $K=10{,}000$-word LAION vocabulary. The coefficient $c_{ik}$ measures the activation of concept $k$ for image $i$ and its scale is independent of a probability simplex. A frozen DINOv3 encoder separately produces a normalized visual embedding $v_i$ of the same image $x_i$. CLIP and SpLiCE formulate a language-aligned hypothesis about a semantic factor and DINO later evaluates the visual compatibility of the relations produced by that hypothesis \citep{simeoni2025dinov3}.

A semantic factor is usually distributed across several dictionary entries. A forest background may activate \textit{forest}, \textit{forests}, \textit{woods}, \textit{vegetation} and \textit{rainforest}. CRP first retains concepts active on between $0.01$ and $0.95$ of the training images. For each retained concept $k$, its dataset-level activation profile is $a_k=(c_{1k},\ldots,c_{Nk})$. Two concepts provide evidence for the same semantic factor (read two concepts have a close meaning) when their text directions are similar and their activation profiles co-occur across the dataset:
\begin{equation}
    s_{\mathrm{text}}(k,\ell)=\cos(d_k,d_\ell),
    \qquad
    s_{\mathrm{coact}}(k,\ell)=\cos(a_k,a_\ell).
    \label{eq:concept_grouping}
\end{equation}
Connected concepts form a semantic group $G$, such as
\begin{equation}
    G=\{\texttt{forest},\texttt{forests},\texttt{woods},
    \texttt{vegetation},\texttt{rainforest}\}.
\end{equation}
The corresponding concept directions form $D_G$. Since several words can describe nearly the same CLIP direction, CRP converts this matrix into a column-orthonormal basis
\begin{equation}
    Q_G=\operatorname{orth}(D_G^\top)
    \label{eq:group_basis}
\end{equation}
The group has now become a geometric subspace. The columns of $Q_G$ are independent axes spanning the joint meaning of the related words.

\paragraph{Removing factor and discovering counterfactual relations.}
CRP uses the group subspace for a representation-space intervention. The projection $Q_GQ_G^\top u_i$ extracts the component of image $i$ aligned with above mentioned group $G$. Subtracting it from the full centered embedding gives
\begin{equation}
    u_i^{-G}=\operatorname{norm}\!\left((I-Q_GQ_G^\top)u_i\right).
    \label{eq:crp_projection}
\end{equation}
This operation aims to keep the core information contained in the SpLiCE reconstruction after removing information from certain concepts and their residuals. For a forest-like group, $u_i^{-G}$ can be read as the CLIP embedding after removing its component along the forest and vegetation subspace.

CRP next finds the top-20 neighbours of every anchor $i$ in the projected space. For a candidate neighbour $j$, the intervention gain compares similarity after and before projection:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta(i,j,G)=\cos(u_i^{-G},u_j^{-G})-
    \cos(u_i,u_j).
    \label{eq:crp_gain}
\end{equation}
A large positive $\Delta(i,j,G)$ indicates that factor $G$ previously separated the two images. Consider two images of compatible birds shown on lake and jungle. Their original CLIP similarity can be low because the backgrounds differ. Projection of the background factor can move them closer and reveal the shared visual content.

This gain supplies a relation hypothesis, which CRP strengthens with three complementary checks. First, a candidate relation should exhibit a meaningful contrast in the intervened concept group. Let
\begin{equation}
    A_i^G = \sum_{k \in G} c_{ik}
\end{equation}
denote the aggregate activation of concept group $G$ for image $i$.
We favor pairs with a substantial activation difference
\begin{equation}
    \left| A_i^G - A_j^G \right|,
\end{equation}
since a positive intervention gain is informative only when the two images initially differ along the semantic factor being removed. The activation contrast therefore establishes that $G$ distinguishes the pair, while the intervention gain tests whether removing $G$ reduces their representational discrepancy.

Second, we require the intervention-induced neighbourhood relation to be reciprocal. If removing $G$ reveals a meaningful similarity between two images, this relation should not arise solely from a one-sided nearest-neighbour match: $i$ should lie near $j$, while $j$ should also rank $i$ among its top-$k$ neighbours.

Third, the relation must be supported by an independent visual representation. We require $i$ and $j$ to also be neighbours in DINO space. This prevents CRP from accepting a relation only because it is induced by the same CLIP/SpLiCE geometry used to construct the intervention. Instead, DINO acts as an independent agreement signal that the revealed relation corresponds to shared visual content.

Together, these checks determine whether a candidate pair is retained. For a
retained relation, we then assign a confidence that reflects both the strength
of the intervention effect and the independent visual agreement:
\begin{equation}
    w_{ij}^{G}
    =
    \Delta(i,j,G)\,
    \phi\!\left(\cos(v_i,v_j)\right),
\end{equation}
where $v_i$ and $v_j$ are the DINO embeddings of images $i$ and $j$ and $\phi$ is a non-negative, non-decreasing function specified in the experimental protocol. Thus, a relation receives high confidence only when removing $G$ substantially increases the similarity of the pair and DINO independently supports their visual similarity.

Pairwise data shows local associations at best. To claim that $G$ captures a systematic relation, CRP looks for a consistent response across a broad set of anchors, with the same pattern recurring across many of them. We summarize the evidence at the group level using four complementary signals: \emph{gain}, measuring the strength of the intervention effect; \emph{coverage}, measuring how broadly the effect occurs across anchors; \emph{agreement}, measuring support from the independent DINO representation; and \emph{alignment}, measuring whether larger activation contrasts in $G$ correspond to larger intervention gains. We additionally penalize \emph{concentration}, preventing a group from receiving a high score only because many relations collapse onto a small number of images. The resulting group score is
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{score}(G)
    =
    \frac{
        \operatorname{gain}(G)\,
        \operatorname{coverage}(G)\,
        \operatorname{agreement}(G)\,
        \operatorname{alignment}(G)
    }{
        \operatorname{concentration}(G)
    }.
\end{equation}

The surviving relations define a sparse teacher graph. Since some images may appear as neighbours of many different anchors, we degree-balance the retained edge confidences before normalization so that a small number of highly connected images do not dominate the teacher signal. For each anchor image $i$, the remaining confidences are then normalized into a probability distribution over its accepted neighbours:
\begin{equation}
    p_T(j \mid i)
    =
    \frac{w_{ij}}
    {\sum_{k \in \mathcal{N}_T(i)} w_{ik}},
    \qquad
    \sum_j p_T(j \mid i)=1.
\end{equation}
The resulting distribution represents the relative strength of the counterfactual relations discovered for anchor $i$ and serves as the relational teacher signal for subsequent self-supervised training.

\paragraph{Transferring the relation graph into the SSL encoder.}
The ``student'' is a ResNet trained jointly with the standard SimCLR objective and the relational CRP objective. Because the latter can only use teacher relations whose endpoints occur in the same mini-batch, we employ a graph-aware batch sampler. Each training image remains an anchor once per epoch. When an anchor has supported neighbours in the teacher graph, the sampler attempts to include at least one such neighbour in the same mini-batch, sampled according to the teacher edge weights. This increases the availability of teacher-supported relations without replacing the ordinary SimCLR training stream. For two augmented views of image $i$, the backbone produces SimCLR embeddings $h_i^{(1)}$ and $h_i^{(2)}$. Their normalized average defines the student feature used by CRP:
\begin{equation}
    s_i=\operatorname{norm}\!\left(
    \operatorname{norm}(h_i^{(1)})+
    \operatorname{norm}(h_i^{(2)})\right).
\end{equation}
This representation summarizes the relation geometry assigned to the underlying training image while retaining the ordinary two-view SimCLR objective for its training. Within mini-batch $B$, the student defines its own relation distribution from
pairwise cosine similarities between the backbone representations. For anchor $i$, the probability assigned to another batch image $j$ is:
\begin{equation}
    p_S^{(B)}(j\mid i)=
    \frac{\exp(\cos(s_i,s_j)/\tau_S)}
    {\sum_{k\in B\setminus\{i\}}\exp(\cos(s_i,s_k)/\tau_S)}.
    \label{eq:crp_student}
\end{equation}
The teacher distribution is restricted to neighbours present in the batch and renormalized to $p_T^{(B)}$. Each supported anchor contributes a confidence-weighted relational loss:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CRP}}=
    \frac{1}{|B_S|}\sum_{i\in B_S}q_i
    D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(p_T^{(B)}(\cdot\mid i)\,\|\,
    p_S^{(B)}(\cdot\mid i)\right),
    \label{eq:crp_relational_loss}
\end{equation}
where $B_S$ denotes the anchors with teacher support in the current mini-batch and $q_i$ is the frozen confidence assigned to anchor $i$ by the teacher graph. The complete training objective is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SimCLR}}
    +\lambda\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CRP}}.
    \label{eq:crp_total}
\end{equation}
The contrastive term trains the standard SimCLR projection head and the CRP term shapes the backbone geometry. The teacher communicates which images became neighbours after a concept intervention. The student learns these relations through its own representation and operates independently of CLIP, SpLiCE and DINO at inference time.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\subsection{Dataset and evaluation protocol}
We evaluate on Waterbirds \citep{sagawa2020groupdro}, which contains 4,795 training images with a strong association between bird type and background. The two target classes and two background attributes define four evaluation groups. SSL pretraining receives the images and their sample identifiers. Target and background annotations enter the pipeline during linear probing and post-hoc graph analysis.

After every 25 SSL epochs, we fit a linear target probe on the frozen backbone for 100 epochs and evaluate it on the validation split. We report average accuracy and worst-group accuracy (WGA). Each result averages the final ten periodic validation probes to reduce variation from a single checkpoint. An auxiliary background probe measures how linearly accessible the spurious attribute remains. The test split is reserved for evaluation of a locked final configuration.

\subsection{Frozen teacher construction}
The teacher cache contains centered OpenCLIP ViT-B/32 features, sparse SpLiCE codes over the 10,000-word LAION vocabulary and DINOv3 ViT-S/16 features. We retain concepts active on $1\%$ to $95\%$ of training images and build semantic groups from text similarity and dataset-level coactivation. For each group, relation discovery searches the top-20 projected CLIP neighbours and checks the candidates against the top-50 DINO neighbours. Random subspaces and shuffled SpLiCE activations calibrate group selection. The final sparse graph keeps at most ten neighbours per anchor and applies indegree balancing. Graph creation is label-free; target and background annotations are loaded afterward for descriptive edge statistics. Appendix~\ref{app:hyperparameters} gives the complete graph configuration.

\subsection{Student training and model selection}
The student is a ResNet-18 with a 128-dimensional SimCLR projection head. We train for 500 epochs with batch size 256 using SGD. The CRP term starts after epoch 10 and reaches its full weight through a ten-epoch linear ramp. The baseline uses the same data order, architecture, augmentations, optimizer, learning-rate schedule, probe schedule and seed. The controlled sweep varies the relational weight $\lambda\in\{0.01,0.025,0.05\}$ and student temperature $\tau_S\in\{0.005,0.01,0.05,0.10,0.20,0.25\}$ over 13 selected combinations. All reported training runs use seed 0 and all model choices use validation metrics. This experiment therefore measures optimization feasibility and hyperparameter sensitivity. Multi-seed confirmation forms the next evaluation stage. Full training and probe settings appear in Appendix~\ref{app:hyperparameters}.

The temperature sweep reveals a clear optimization pattern. Moderate student temperatures from $0.05$ to $0.25$ produce consistent gains for $\lambda=0.05$. The sharpest distribution at $\tau_S=0.005$ yields WGA near $33\%$ for both tested weights. This sensitivity agrees with the role of Equation~\ref{eq:crp_student}: a very small temperature concentrates probability on one in-batch neighbour and amplifies teacher-edge errors. Softer student distributions transfer a broader part of the teacher neighbourhood.

The auxiliary background probe reaches $91.99\%$ average accuracy for the best CRP run and $91.58\%$ for SimCLR. These values support a geometry-level interpretation of the result. CRP improves target group balance and background information remains linearly accessible. The observed effect lies in the organization of image neighbourhoods.

\subsection{The frozen graph supplies narrow target-consistent relations}
The teacher graph covers $85.42\%$ of training anchors. A post-hoc label audit shows that $94.65\%$ of its edges connect images with the same target class. However, only $0.96\%$ of these same-target relations connect images with opposite backgrounds. For comparison, raw CLIP neighbours achieve $94.46\%$ same-target precision and $2.81\%$ opposite-background coverage, while DINO neighbours achieve $92.48\%$ and $1.09\%$, respectively.

These results show that CRP constructs highly target-consistent relations, but the current graph largely remains within the existing background structure. It does not yet preferentially discover same-target relations that cross the spurious background attribute. This highlights the limited ability of the current frozen graph to discover relations that cross background variation.

\subsection{Evidence boundary}
The present experiments establish that the CRP objective trains successfully and can improve both average accuracy and WGA under a matched protocol. It also identifies a stable high-performing temperature range at seed 0. Statistical claims across random initializations require the planned multi-seed Waterbirds evaluation. Evaluation on additional spurious-correlation benchmarks will measure dataset transfer. Student runs trained from raw-CLIP, DINO-only and shuffled teacher graphs will further separate the contribution of concept projection from relational distillation itself.

\section{Limitations}
The current empirical study covers Waterbirds and one SSL seed. Hyperparameter selection uses validation WGA and the reported best configuration therefore represents preliminary model-development evidence. The test split remains reserved for a locked configuration. Multi-seed replication and additional datasets are required for statistical and cross-dataset conclusions.

Feature relevance is task-dependent. CRP assumes that removing a selected factor restores relations supported by an independent SSL model. CLIP and DINO can encode similar shortcuts and concept groups can entangle class information with context. Full subspace projection may therefore change both. Sparse minority groups can also limit the supply of cross-background relations despite graph balancing. Post-hoc graph precision and coverage use hidden annotations solely for evaluation and all graph construction thresholds remain label-free.

\section{Conclusion}
SpLiCE-CRP turns interpretable concept projection into relational supervision for a standalone SSL encoder. A preliminary Waterbirds sweep improves average validation accuracy by $4.55$ points and WGA by $8.28$ points over matched SimCLR at seed 0. Performance is strongest with moderately soft student relation distributions. The frozen audit shows high target consistency together with limited neighbourhood turnover and cross-background coverage. These results establish the feasibility of concept-projected relational pretraining and define clear targets for multi-seed validation and richer relation discovery.

\author{Anonymous Authors}

\begin{abstract}
SpLiCE-CRP constructs relational supervision for an image encoder using frozen
OpenCLIP embeddings and sparse language-aligned SpLiCE codes. Concept groups
specify subspaces whose removal reveals candidate image relations. Positive
intervention gain, activation contrast and residual concept agreement determine
pair evidence. Random-subspace and shuffled-activation controls calibrate group
selection. A sparse teacher graph supplies graph-aware batches and a
confidence-weighted KL term alongside ordinary SimCLR. An optional spatial
extension derives image-specific support in the same SpLiCE vocabulary. The
completed Waterbirds study evaluates the ordinary pipeline with four matched
controls and two student seeds. At SSL epoch 500, SpLiCE-CRP reaches mean validation
accuracy of 51.67\% and worst-group accuracy of 48.07\%. The matched raw-CLIP
teacher reaches 52.46\% and 46.79\%, respectively. These observations motivate
further evaluation of graph construction, downstream generalization and
student-seed sensitivity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Unlabeled image collections contain correlations between objects and their
surroundings. A contrastive representation can encode both sources of variation.
SpLiCE provides sparse non-negative coordinates in a language-aligned dictionary
\citep{bhalla2024splice}. These coordinates support an inspectable intervention:
a group of related concepts defines a subspace and its removal changes image
neighbourhoods in the frozen embedding space.

SpLiCE-CRP uses this change to construct a fixed teacher graph. The student learns
from augmented images through SimCLR and relational distillation. The graph also
controls batch composition. At inference, the trained backbone supplies features
for a downstream classifier. The current control study isolates the contributions
of batch composition, generic CLIP transfer and concept-projected relations.

\section{Problem Setting}
Let $\mathcal{D}=\{x_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$ be the unlabeled training images with stable
sample identifiers. Graph discovery and SSL consume these images and frozen
representations. Target labels $y_i$, background attributes $a_i$ and evaluation
groups $g_i=(y_i,a_i)$ enter supervised downstream evaluation and post-hoc diagnosis.
The discovery cache validator rejects annotation fields. All graph configurations
are fixed before group-wise downstream metrics are inspected.

\section{Current Architecture}
\subsection{Frozen representations and semantic groups}
Frozen OpenCLIP produces image embeddings $z_i$. The cached image mean $\mu$
defines centered normalized embeddings
\begin{equation}
 u_i=\operatorname{norm}(z_i-\mu).
\end{equation}
SpLiCE supplies codes $c_i\in\mathbb{R}^{K}_{\geq0}$ and a normalized text dictionary
$D\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times d}$. The sparse reconstruction is $c_iD$.
Cache rows, vocabulary entries and dictionary directions have explicit alignment.

The ordinary pipeline filters concepts by their occurrence frequency. Lexical
families are joined and additional pairs are joined when text similarity and
coactivation cosine exceed their configured thresholds. Connected components
form flat concept groups. For a group $G$, an SVD of its dictionary directions
provides an orthonormal column basis $Q_G$. The numerical rank determines the
projection dimension.

\subsection{Concept projection and relation evidence}
The projection acts on the full centered embedding:
\begin{equation}
 u_i^{-G}=\operatorname{norm}\!\left(u_i-Q_GQ_G^\top u_i\right),
 \label{eq:projection}
\end{equation}
where embeddings are written as column vectors in this equation. Projected
nearest neighbours provide candidate donors. Their gain is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_{ij}^{G}=\cos(u_i^{-G},u_j^{-G})-\cos(u_i,u_j).
\end{equation}
Group activation is $A_i^G=\sum_{k\in G}c_{ik}$. Candidate edges require a positive
gain above the configured minimum and an activation difference above the
configured quantile of positive-gain candidates.

With the residual gate enabled, the remaining code coordinates define
\begin{equation}
 R_{ij}^{-G}=\cos(c_i^{-G},c_j^{-G}),\qquad R_{ij}^{-G}\geq\tau_R.
\end{equation}
Zero residual norm receives similarity zero. Accepted pair evidence is
\begin{equation}
 e_{ij}^{G}=\Delta_{ij}^{G}\left(\tfrac12+\tfrac12R_{ij}^{-G}\right).
\end{equation}
The group score combines median accepted gain, anchor coverage, mean residual
agreement and the non-negative correlation between activation difference and gain.
A hubness penalty uses destination indegree Gini and maximum indegree:
\begin{equation}
 S(G)=\frac{\operatorname{medianGain}(G)\operatorname{coverage}(G)
 \operatorname{agreement}(G)\operatorname{alignment}(G)}
 {1+\operatorname{Gini}(G)+\operatorname{maxIndegree}(G)/N}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Null calibration and sparse teacher graph}
Equal-rank random subspaces and shuffled group activations produce null scores.
Their pooled configured quantile is $T_G$. A group passes when it has sufficient
coverage and $S(G)>T_G$. Its evidence is scaled by
\begin{equation}
 r_G=\min\!\left(1,\frac{\max(0,S(G)-T_G)}{\max(|S(G)|,\epsilon)}\right),
 \qquad \tilde e_{ij}^{G}=r_Ge_{ij}^{G}.
\end{equation}
The configured group cap keeps the largest null-excess scores after the audit.
Duplicate directed edges retain
the strongest evidence. Outgoing top-$k$ pruning and an absolute destination
indegree cap yield a sparse graph. Supported rows define
\begin{equation}
 p_T(j\mid i)=\frac{\tilde e_{ij}}{\sum_k\tilde e_{ik}},\qquad
 q_i=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}\max_j\tilde e_{ij}.
\end{equation}
The row probabilities specify relative donor preferences and $q_i$ carries
absolute evidence strength. Unsupported anchors have zero relational contribution.
The trainer supports an empty-graph SimCLR fallback. The matched control runner
requires a nonempty graph to preserve the intended experimental contrasts.

\subsection{Graph-aware student training}
The sampler visits each image exactly once per SSL epoch and places a sampled
available teacher neighbour in the same batch when possible. A ResNet backbone
produces two augmented-view features $h_i^{(1)}$ and $h_i^{(2)}$. The relational
representation is
\begin{equation}
 s_i=\operatorname{norm}\!\left[\operatorname{norm}(h_i^{(1)})+
 \operatorname{norm}(h_i^{(2)})\right].
\end{equation}
A softmax over backbone similarities defines $p_S^B(j\mid i)$ in batch $B$ with
temperature $\tau_S$. Occurrences of the anchor sample are masked. Restricting
$p_T$ to batch donors and renormalizing gives $p_T^B$. The objective is
\begin{align}
 \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{graph}}&=\frac{1}{|B_S|}\sum_{i\in B_S}q_i
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(p_T^B(\cdot\mid i)\,\|\,p_S^B(\cdot\mid i)\right),\\
 \mathcal{L}(t)&=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SimCLR}}+
 \lambda(t)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{graph}}.
\end{align}
Here $B_S$ contains anchors with an in-batch teacher donor. SimCLR uses its
projection head and the two views of each image define its positive pair.
In the completed controls, KL starts after epoch 10 and warms up over ten epochs
to weight 2. The weight then remains constant through epoch 500.

\subsection{Optional spatial extension}
CRPv4 supports a separate spatial evidence artifact aligned with the exact cached
sample IDs and SpLiCE vocabulary. Frozen patch evidence supports four variants:
vanilla patchwise, vanilla with slots, SCLIP patchwise and SCLIP with slots.
Image-specific evidence rescales existing SpLiCE activations before grouping and
relation construction. The original frozen cache is preserved. A configurable
floor controls attenuation and optional frequency scaling changes relative
concept support. Row rescaling preserves each image's original total code mass.
Zero input coordinates remain zero under this multiplicative
operation. The spatial variants are available for subsequent controlled studies.
The completed four-arm experiment uses ordinary SpLiCE codes.

\section{Evaluation Protocol}
The completed Waterbirds controls use ResNet-18-large, 500 SSL epochs, batch size
128 and SGD with learning rate 0.01, momentum 0.9 and weight decay $10^{-4}$.
SimCLR temperature is 0.05 and relational temperature is 0.25. Student seeds are
1 and 2. Both seeds share graph seed 0 and identical graph fingerprints.

Every 25 SSL epochs, a frozen-feature logistic probe is fitted on the
\texttt{ds\_train} group-balanced training subset. It contains 56 examples per
group in these runs. Feature standardization uses training statistics. Full-batch
float64 L-BFGS minimizes mean cross-entropy with L2 coefficient 0.001 on classifier
weights. Convergence requires ten consecutive external optimization epochs with
maximum absolute training-objective gradient below $10^{-6}$. Each external step
allows up to 20 inner iterations; a safety limit of 200 external epochs applies.
Reported Avg. and WGA average the final ten converged probe optimization epochs.
The SSL epoch identifies the representation being evaluated.

Validation has group counts 467, 466, 133 and 133 in $(y,a)$ order 00, 01, 10 and
11. Each probe records every group accuracy and count in local JSON and W\&B.
An auxiliary background probe measures background predictability. Test evaluation
is reserved for a fixed method and protocol.

\section{Controls and Interpretation}
The raw-CLIP teacher uses centered-CLIP neighbours with the same supported anchors,
outgoing row budgets, row weight profiles and anchor confidence as CRP. Both
teacher graphs obey the same absolute indegree cap. Each graph can still induce
its own batches. CRP sampler-only uses the CRP graph with KL weight zero.
The additional raw-CLIP sampler-only arm is available for isolating that teacher's
batch-composition effect.

CRP improves WGA over SimCLR by 2.36 and 5.57 percentage points for seeds 1 and 2.
Compared with raw CLIP, its WGA gains are 1.50 and 1.07 points; accuracy changes
are $-1.75$ and $+0.17$ points. The mean result therefore expresses an accuracy
tradeoff. Two student seeds provide preliminary evidence conditional on one
frozen graph. Background probe accuracy remains approximately 91--93\%.
These measurements motivate checks of downstream generalization and further
paired student-seed experiments.

\section{Graph Screening and Research Limits}
The existing frozen search evaluates nine text/coactivation threshold pairs.
Its group screen collapses each group into a text prototype and measures fidelity
to the original sparse reconstruction. This diagnostic evaluates prototype
compression, while the CRP intervention in Eq.~\ref{eq:projection} uses the full
group subspace. The completed search obtains roughly 76.7\% reconstruction
coverage at cosine 0.9 against a requested coverage of 99\%. Compression gains
range from 0.2\% to 4.2\% against a minimum of 5\%. All candidates are rejected
before full graph construction. Mini-intervention measurements are absent because
the reconstruction gate prevents their execution.

A proposed search revision separates reconstruction diagnostics from graph
admissibility and evaluates intervention evidence directly. This revision remains
an implementation plan. The existing seed-1/2 study uses the locked canonical
graph. Its measurements characterize that graph, its sampler and the student
objective together. Subsequent graph studies require per-graph matched teachers,
fixed evaluation and explicit graph fingerprints.

Concept groups can contain both object and background information. Projection
can remove several factors simultaneously. Null excess measures a score relative
to synthetic controls; downstream robustness requires direct evaluation.
Useful diagnosis includes support, donor availability, group-wise relation types,
absolute confidence and realized minibatch exposure. Post-hoc group annotations
remain isolated from graph selection and training weights.

\section{Conclusion}
SpLiCE-CRP transfers a frozen concept-projected relation graph through graph-aware
batching and confidence-weighted backbone KL alongside SimCLR. The current
Waterbirds controls show a preliminary WGA improvement and an accuracy tradeoff
relative to a matched raw-CLIP teacher. Direct graph diagnostics and further
matched experiments will clarify the contributions of concept grouping,
relation evidence and student optimization.

\title{SpLiCE-CRP: Concept-Projected Relational Pretraining for Label-Free Spurious Robustness}
\author{Anonymous Authors}

\begin{abstract}
Self-supervised representations can encode accidental correlations even without
labels. We introduce \emph{SpLiCE-CRP}, or Concept-Projected Relational
Pretraining, a label-free method that converts semantic concept interventions into
relational supervision for an independent SSL encoder. Frozen OpenCLIP and SpLiCE
features propose language-readable concept groups. CRP removes each group subspace
from the full centered CLIP embedding and measures which image relations become
stronger. Reciprocal projected neighbours and frozen DINO features guard semantic
compatibility; matched random subspaces and shuffled concept activations calibrate
selection. Null-calibrated evidence becomes a sparse, degree-controlled teacher
graph. A ResNet is trained from images with SimCLR and confidence-weighted
relational distillation on backbone similarities; graph edges can additionally
supply weighted contrastive positives. Target, spurious-attribute, and group
annotations are excluded from discovery, graph construction, and SSL training.
Initial seed-0 Waterbirds results improve average and worst-group validation
accuracy, while multi-seed validation and non-concept controls remain necessary.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Removing labels from pretraining does not remove correlations from images. In
Waterbirds, bird type is correlated with background, so a contrastive encoder can
organize its representation around water and land context without observing either
annotation. A label-free robust method therefore needs alternative evidence about
which variations should exert less influence on representation geometry.

SpLiCE expresses a frozen CLIP embedding with sparse non-negative activations over
a language-aligned dictionary \citep{bhalla2024splice}. This creates an intervention
interface: a word group defines a semantic subspace, and projecting it out asks
which image relations become visible when that factor has less influence. Such a
relation is useful only if it survives independent checks and recurs systematically.

SpLiCE-CRP has two stages. A frozen audit constructs a sparse teacher graph from
concept interventions, independent visual agreement, and label-free null controls.
A trainable ResNet then receives this graph through a graph-aware sampler,
confidence-weighted relational KL, and optionally weighted positives in SimCLR. At
inference only the ResNet backbone remains; OpenCLIP, SpLiCE, DINO, and the graph
are no longer required.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
 \item a language-readable, label-free procedure that proposes semantic factor
 subspaces and tests them by full-space intervention;
 \item a sparse teacher graph whose edge and anchor confidence preserve the
 absolute strength of null-calibrated frozen evidence;
 \item a combined SSL objective that transfers graph geometry into the backbone
 while preserving SimCLR and optionally resolves graph-neighbour/negative conflict;
 \item a Waterbirds pilot with diagnostics that separate graph coverage, absolute
 confidence, unweighted KL, and effective weighted KL.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Language-aligned concepts.}
TCAV introduced concept directions \citep{kim2018tcav}, ACE demonstrated automatic
concept discovery \citep{ghorbani2019ace}, and Concept Bottleneck Models made
concepts explicit in prediction \citep{koh2020concept}. SpLiCE directly underlies
CRP because its sparse coordinates and text dictionary inhabit the CLIP space in
which we intervene \citep{bhalla2024splice}.

\paragraph{Spurious-correlation mitigation.}
Group DRO uses group annotations \citep{sagawa2020groupdro}; EIIL and Just Train
Twice reduce annotation requirements but retain target-dependent signals
\citep{creager2021eiil,liu2021jtt}. CoBalT uses object-centric concepts in supervised
balancing \citep{arefin2024cobalt}, while DIAL removes sparse directions inside a
vision-language model \citep{yalavarthi2026dial}. CRP instead uses frozen concept
removal to create relations for a separately trained image encoder.

\paragraph{Relational self-supervision.}
NNCLR shows that cross-image neighbours can supervise contrastive learning
\citep{dwibedi2021nnclr}. Correct-N-Contrast and Cross-Variant SSL construct robust
relations using stronger task information or predefined interventions
\citep{zhang2022cnc,yadav2026crossvariant}. Generic label-free methods such as
learning-speed-aware sampling and spectral regularization remain essential
same-setting baselines \citep{zhu2023lassl,ghanooni2025spectral}.

\section{Problem Setting}
Let $\mathcal{D}=\{(x_i,y_i,a_i)\}_{i=1}^{N}$, where $y_i$ is the downstream
target and $a_i$ a spurious attribute; evaluation groups are $g_i=(y_i,a_i)$.
During graph discovery and SSL training, CRP receives only images $x_i$ and stable
sample identifiers. Labels, attributes, and groups are reserved for downstream
linear probing and explicitly post-hoc diagnostics.

After SSL training, a linear classifier is fitted on the frozen backbone. We report
average accuracy and worst-group accuracy (WGA). CRP does not assume that nuisance
factors are universally identifiable from unlabeled data. Its testable inductive
bias is narrower: removing a useful nuisance candidate should repeatedly reveal
relations supported by a separate visual geometry without creating excessive hubs.

\section{SpLiCE-CRP}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Frozen cache and semantic groups}
Frozen OpenCLIP produces normalized image embeddings $z_i$. With the SpLiCE image
mean $\mu$, CRP forms full centered embeddings
\begin{equation}u_i=\operatorname{norm}(z_i-\mu).\end{equation}
SpLiCE supplies non-negative sparse codes $c_i\in\mathbb{R}^{K}_{\geq0}$ and a
normalized text dictionary $D=[d_1^\top;\ldots;d_K^\top]$ such that
$u_i\approx D^\top c_i$. Frozen DINO independently produces normalized visual
embeddings $v_i$. Cache rows are aligned by sample identifier, and the cache
validator rejects hidden annotation fields.

CRP filters concepts by unlabeled activation frequency. It joins trivial lexical
variants and joins other concepts only when both dictionary-direction similarity
and activation-profile co-occurrence pass fixed thresholds. For group $G$, SVD
produces a stable orthonormal basis
\begin{equation}Q_G=\operatorname{orth}(D_G^\top).\end{equation}
Near-collinear directions are dropped, so the intervention depends on the subspace
rather than the number of synonymous words.

\subsection{Full-space concept intervention}
The canonical intervention removes the group span from the \emph{full} centered
CLIP vector:
\begin{equation}
 u_i^{-G}=\operatorname{norm}\!\left[u_i-(u_iQ_G)Q_G^\top\right].
 \label{eq:projection}
\end{equation}
The sparse code proposes the interpretable subspace, but subtraction is not limited
to its reconstruction. Components aligned with $G$ are therefore removed even if
they remain in the SpLiCE residual.

CRP finds top-$k_P$ projected neighbours. For candidate $j$, intervention gain is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_{ij}^{G}=\cos(u_i^{-G},u_j^{-G})-\cos(u_i,u_j).
 \label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
A pair is eligible only when gain exceeds a minimum, summed group activations
$A_i^G=\sum_{k\in G}c_{ik}$ differ by at least a group-specific unlabeled quantile,
the projected relation is reciprocal, and $j$ lies in the raw-DINO neighbour set
of $i$. DINO guards semantic compatibility; it does not define the intervention.

Accepted-edge evidence is
\begin{equation}
 e_{ij}^{G}=\Delta_{ij}^{G}
 \left(\tfrac12+\tfrac12\max(0,\cos(v_i,v_j))\right).
 \label{eq:edge-confidence}
\end{equation}
For each group, CRP combines median accepted gain, anchor coverage, mean DINO
agreement, non-negative activation-difference/gain correlation, and hubness:
\begin{equation}
 S(G)=\frac{\operatorname{medianGain}(G)\operatorname{coverage}(G)
 \operatorname{agreement}(G)\operatorname{alignment}(G)}
 {1+\operatorname{Gini}(G)+\operatorname{maxIndegree}(G)/N}.
 \label{eq:group-score}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Null calibration and teacher graph}
CRP compares $S(G)$ with equal-rank random-subspace scores and scores obtained after
shuffling group activations. Let $T_G$ be a fixed quantile of their combined null.
A group is retained only if it passes minimum coverage and $S(G)>T_G$. Its pair
evidence is scaled by relative null margin:
\begin{equation}
 r_G=\min\!\left(1,\frac{\max(0,S(G)-T_G)}{\max(|S(G)|,\epsilon)}\right),
 \qquad \tilde e_{ij}^{G}=r_Ge_{ij}^{G}.
 \label{eq:null-margin}
\end{equation}
An optional post-gate cap keeps groups with largest label-free null excess.

For duplicate directed pairs, CRP keeps the strongest group evidence. It retains
at most $k_G$ outgoing edges per anchor and applies a hard destination-indegree cap.
Remaining confidences are row-normalized:
\begin{equation}
 p_T(j\mid i)=\frac{\tilde e_{ij}}
 {\sum_{k\in\mathcal{N}_T(i)}\tilde e_{ik}}.
 \label{eq:teacher}
\end{equation}
Relative teacher preference is separated from absolute anchor confidence,
\begin{equation}
 q_i=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}\max_j\tilde e_{ij}.
 \label{eq:absolute-confidence}
\end{equation}
Thus row normalization cannot make a uniformly weak graph as influential as a
strong graph. If no group passes, training explicitly reduces to matched SimCLR.

\subsection{Graph-aware relational SSL}
A graph-aware batch sampler visits each image as an anchor once per epoch and, when
possible, includes one neighbour sampled from $p_T(\cdot\mid i)$. It increases the
number of supported minibatch anchors without replacing the ordinary SSL stream.

For backbone features $h_i^{(1)},h_i^{(2)}$ of two views, CRP uses
\begin{equation}
 s_i=\operatorname{norm}\left[\operatorname{norm}(h_i^{(1)})+
 \operatorname{norm}(h_i^{(2)})\right].
\end{equation}
Within batch $B$, the student relation distribution is
\begin{equation}
 p_S^B(j\mid i)=\frac{\exp(\cos(s_i,s_j)/\tau_S)}
 {\sum_{k\in B:\,k\not\equiv i}\exp(\cos(s_i,s_k)/\tau_S)}.
 \label{eq:student}
\end{equation}
All occurrences of the same sample are masked. Restricting and renormalizing the
teacher to neighbours present in $B$ gives $p_T^B$. Supported anchors contribute
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rel}}=\frac{1}{|B_S|}\sum_{i\in B_S}q_i
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(p_T^B(\cdot\mid i)\,\|\,
 p_S^B(\cdot\mid i)\right).
 \label{eq:relational}
\end{equation}
The KL acts directly on backbone geometry; SimCLR uses its projection head.

Optionally, graph-linked samples become weighted additional NT-Xent positives with
symmetric weights derived from $q_i p_T(j\mid i)$. This avoids treating a teacher
neighbour only as a SimCLR negative while KL attracts it. Denote the resulting loss
by $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SimCLR}}^{+G}$; disabling the option recovers standard
SimCLR. The complete implemented objective is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{L}(t)=\alpha\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SimCLR}}^{+G}
 +\lambda(t)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rel}}.
 \label{eq:total}
\end{equation}
After a pure-SimCLR start, $\lambda(t)$ ramps linearly to its configured value. An
optional late linear decay returns it to zero. Graph positives follow the same
active interval. Setting $\alpha=0$ defines a diagnostic KL-only ablation.

\section{Experimental Setup}
Waterbirds contains 4,795 training images and four bird/background groups
\citep{sagawa2020groupdro}. SSL sees only images and identifiers. Every 25 SSL
epochs, a 100-epoch linear probe is fitted on the frozen backbone and evaluated on
validation data. Reported Avg. and WGA are means over the final ten probes. A
separate background probe measures accessibility of the spurious attribute.

The student is ResNet-18-large with a 128-dimensional head, trained for 500 epochs
with batch 256, SGD learning rate 0.01, momentum 0.9, weight decay $10^{-4}$, and
SimCLR temperature 0.05. Learning rate drops by 0.1 at epochs 350, 400, and 450.
Resolved configuration, runtime versions, SSL diagnostics, and evaluation metrics
are retained in W\&B.

The newest bounded seed-0 graph uses minimum group size 2, text threshold 0.70,
coactivation threshold 0.20, and at most 12 groups after the null gate. It uses
activation-difference quantile 0.85, minimum gain $5\times10^{-4}$, 32 trials per
null family, top-20 projected neighbours, top-50 DINO support, and at most five
outgoing edges. The graph has 6,268 edges, 3,060 supported anchors (63.82\%),
effective donor count 2,109.75, and maximum indegree five.

The completed loss-isolation runs disable graph positives and late decay, activate
KL after epoch 10, ramp for ten epochs, and use $\tau_S=0.25$. They differ only in
$\lambda\in\{1,2\}$. Two $\lambda=1$ temperature checks are still running and are
not treated as final evidence.

Increasing $\lambda$ from 1 to 2 improves Avg. by 1.70 points and WGA by 3.37
points. Relative to matched SimCLR, the stronger run improves Avg. by 5.21 points
and WGA by 9.88 points. Training remains numerically stable.

Diagnostics explain the effective scale. At the end of both runs, roughly 44\% of
minibatch anchors have teacher support and mean supported-anchor confidence is
about 0.00232. Unweighted KL is about 5.2, but confidence reduces it to about 0.012
before the scheduled multiplier. Thus $\lambda=2$ contributes about 0.024 to the
final loss: active but smaller than SimCLR. The best run's background probe remains
high at about 91.9\%. CRP has therefore not demonstrated removal of background
information; the supported statement is that it changes representation geometry
and improves group balance in this seed.

\section{Evidence Boundary and Limitations}
These results are mechanism-finding pilots. All runs use seed 0, and graph/loss
settings were developed with validation feedback. Paired seeds are required before
claiming stability. Improvement over SimCLR does not isolate language-aligned
concept intervention. Required controls include raw-CLIP and DINO-only relational
distillation, random-subspace projection, shuffled SpLiCE codes, and a strong fully
self-supervised baseline including spectral regularization.

Core and nuisance factors are not universally identifiable from task-agnostic
unlabeled data. CLIP and DINO may share shortcuts, and concept subspaces can mix
target and context information. Full projection may therefore remove both. Sparse
minority groups can limit cross-context donors; dense graphs can create false
relations or hubs. Finally, absolute confidence makes numerical loss weights
non-comparable across graph calibrations. Graph coverage, anchor confidence,
supported-batch fraction, unweighted KL, weighted KL, and scheduled weight must be
reported together.

\section{Conclusion}
SpLiCE-CRP uses language-aligned subspace removal as a frozen experiment and
transfers relations that survive reciprocal-neighbour, DINO, null, and hubness
checks. The implementation separates relative teacher weights from absolute
confidence, applies graph-aware backbone KL, and can integrate graph neighbours as
weighted SimCLR positives under an explicit schedule. The strongest completed
bounded-graph pilot reaches 54.15\% Avg. and 50.54\% WGA on Waterbirds. Paired seeds
and the full non-concept control suite are required to establish stability and the
causal value of the concept mechanism.

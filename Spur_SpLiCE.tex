\begin{abstract}
Can an interpretable frozen concept model teach a separately trained self-supervised encoder to ignore unknown spurious factors? We study this question through a sequence of controlled Waterbirds experiments using Sparse Linear Concept Embeddings (SpLiCE) from frozen OpenCLIP and a ResNet18-large trained with SimCLR. The completed evidence is mostly negative but diagnostic. Concept-routed augmentation and conditional correlation regularization do not reliably improve worst-group (WG) validation accuracy over matched SimCLR; the best mean change is only $+0.45$ percentage points and semantic routing loses to shuffled routing on all three paired seeds. A target-label-assisted intervention-utility selector improves a frozen SpLiCE concept bottleneck by $+0.75$ WG points, far below a privileged group-aware oracle at $+9.02$ and its edited-teacher SSL pilot does not beat unedited CLIP distillation.

These failures motivate SpLiCE-CRP, a stricter label-free proposal. It groups language-aligned concepts into semantic subspaces, projects each subspace out of the full centered CLIP embedding, retains only relations independently supported by frozen DINO features and constructs a soft counterfactual graph for relational distillation into the SimCLR backbone. The implemented frozen audit rejects the current graph: median Jaccard@20 is $0.965$ and only $0.96\%$ of same-target edges cross backgrounds versus $2.81\%$ for raw CLIP kNN. Consequently, CRP training was not launched. However, a one-seed privileged relational oracle improves WG accuracy from $40.66\%$ to $45.39\%$, suggesting that relation transfer can help when counterfactual pairs are correct. The present contribution is therefore a reproducible hypothesis, a set of strong falsification controls and a precise localization of the remaining bottleneck---label-free discovery of useful counterfactual relations---rather than a claim of state-of-the-art performance.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Self-supervised learning (SSL) removes class labels from pretraining, but not shortcuts from the data. On Waterbirds, a SimCLR representation can support reasonable average classification while retaining a large gap between birds observed in common and uncommon backgrounds. Standard image augmentations provide implicit semantic interventions, while purely geometric regularizers provide an underspecified target for feature suppression.

SpLiCE decomposes CLIP embeddings into sparse non-negative coordinates over language-aligned text concepts \citep{bhalla2024splice}. This suggests a useful interface: a frozen concept model may identify factors such as vegetation, water, colour, texture, or demographic appearance, while a separate SSL encoder learns an invariant representation. The central difficulty is not merely finding interpretable words. Without labels, the method must identify which factor to intervene on and transfer the resulting invariance into a new encoder.

We investigated this problem in four stages. First, group-aware SpLiCE concepts routed stronger augmentations or regularized feature--concept correlations. Second, SpLiCE Intervention Utility (SpLiCE-IU) removed group metadata but still used target labels to select coordinates by held-out error repair. Third, a hard-positive CRP formulation was rejected because one mined neighbour is fragile and can create minority-image hubs. Finally, SpLiCE-CRP v2 replaces hard positives with a label-free, confidence-weighted counterfactual relation graph obtained by full-space concept projection and independent DINO agreement.

The experiments do not (yet) validate CRP v2, however they aim to answer three following questions: \emph{Is useful signal present?} Yes: a privileged concept oracle improves frozen-model WG accuracy by about nine points.
\emph{Do the simple transfer interfaces work?} No: routing, correlation regularization and IU editing fail their matched controls.
\emph{Can a relational objective help if the relations are correct?} A one-seed oracle gives a positive diagnostic, while the current label-free graph fails before training. This separation makes the next research decision quite unclear.

Our current contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item controlled negative results for concept-routed augmentation, correlation regularization, label-assisted intervention utility and representation editing;
    \item SpLiCE-CRP v2, a proposed label-free interface that converts concept interventions into a soft teacher graph for an independent SSL encoder;
    \item a frozen-audit gate showing that the present CRP graph is operationally too weak, plus an oracle relational experiment that localizes the main bottleneck to relation discovery rather than ruling out relational transfer.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work and Positioning}
\paragraph{Concept representations and mitigation.}
SpLiCE provides sparse, language-aligned decompositions of CLIP and supports interpretation and representation editing \citep{bhalla2024splice}; our goal is to transfer concept-conditioned invariance into a different encoder. CoBalT discovers object-centric concepts and balances supervised classifier training without group annotations \citep{arefin2024cobalt}. Bias Leaves a Gradient Trail uses class labels and model errors to rank activation concepts, then suppresses directions in a frozen classifier \citep{vitry2026gradient}. DIAL/DIAL+ discovers sparse-autoencoder directions and edits a deployed VLM representation without class or group labels \citep{yalavarthi2026dial}. Our setting differs because OpenCLIP/SpLiCE is a frozen teacher, whereas the evaluated representation is the independently trained ResNet.

\paragraph{Spurious-robust SSL.}
LA-SSL changes SSL sampling according to learning dynamics and feature-space augmentation methods explicitly diversify representations \citep{zhu2023lassl,hamidieh2024views}. These approaches improve robustness by broadening the variation seen during training, with the intervention defined by sampling rules or feature-space geometry. CRP moves the intervention to the concept level: language-aligned groups specify the factor being changed and allow a direct test of whether concept-conditioned variation adds value beyond raw geometry.

\paragraph{Counterfactual positives.}
NNCLR uses nearest neighbours as additional positives but does not condition retrieval on an interpretable intervention \citep{dwibedi2021nnclr}. Cross-Variant SSL generates background-randomized images with segmentation and diffusion, then treats variants as positives before supervised GroupDRO fine-tuning \citep{yadav2026crossvariant}. It is an informative upper reference, since it assumes a foreground/background intervention, generates new imformation (pixels) and later consumes group supervision. CRP instead attempts a representation-space intervention without a predefined foreground/background decomposition or group labels.

\section{Problem Setting}
For image $x_i$, target $y_i$, spurious attribute $a_i$ and group $g_i=(y_i,a_i)$, the trainable encoder $f_\theta$ is optimized on two stochastic views with the SimCLR NT-Xent loss \citep{chen2020simclr}. Worst-group accuracy (WGA) is used only for post-hoc evaluation.

The proposed CRP v2 pipeline is strictly label-free during concept discovery and SSL training. It may use only unlabeled training images, frozen OpenCLIP/SpLiCE/DINO representations, sample identifiers and statistics computed from these representations. It never uses target labels $y_i$, spurious attributes $a_i$, group identifiers $g_i$, group-balanced sampling, or WGA to select hyperparameters. Labels and group annotations are introduced only afterward, for downstream linear probing, WGA evaluation, post-hoc diagnostics, or explicitly marked oracle experiments. For this reason, legacy group ranking and SpLiCE-IU are reported as privileged baselines.

\section{Methods Investigated}
\subsection{Frozen SpLiCE representation}
A frozen OpenCLIP ViT-B/32 maps $x_i$ to normalized $z_i\in\mathbb{R}^{d_c}$. With dataset mean $\mu$ and a text dictionary $D\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times d_c}$, SpLiCE approximates the normalized centered embedding using a sparse non-negative code $c_i$:
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{norm}(z_i-\mu)\approx c_iD, \qquad c_i\geq 0.
\end{equation}
We use the 10,000-word LAION vocabulary and $\ell_1$ penalty of $0.25$. Frozen features and sample identifiers are cached once, CRP caches explicitly reject label or group fields.

\subsection{Legacy interfaces: routing and correlation removal}
This was the main experimental branch developed in July. The trainable model was a ResNet18-large trained from scratch with SimCLR; OpenCLIP and SpLiCE were used only as a frozen concept analyser. The CLIP embedding and the selected SpLiCE coordinates were computed once and cached, so every training run used the same concept scores and the same image ordering. The initial selector compared concept weights across the four Waterbirds groups after conditioning on the bird label. For concept $k$, with group mean $\mu_{a,y,k}$, it measured a background contrast
\begin{equation}
S_k=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{Y}|}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}
\left|\mu_{1,y,k}-\mu_{0,y,k}\right|,
\end{equation}
and subtracted a target contrast and a class-instability term,
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Score}_k=S_k-T_k-I_k, \qquad
T_k=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}
\left|\mu_{a,1,k}-\mu_{a,0,k}\right|.
\end{equation}
The broad sweep used ten automatically ranked words. The routing-control series added two safeguards: lexical deduplication and a requirement that the signed background effect point in the same direction for both bird classes. Its resulting Top-5 list was \texttt{bamboo, forests, whale, rainforest, raven}, which already showed that the concept proxy mixed background words with bird- and activity-related words.

For routing, the selected coordinates were reduced to one per-image score, $r_i=\max_k c_{ik}$ (the broad sweep also supported a mean reduction). An automatically calibrated training quantile $q$ determined which images received a stronger second view. Images above the threshold produced one standard and one strong view; the remaining images produced two standard views. At $q=0.75$, semantic, shuffled-score and matched-random controls each routed exactly 1,199 of 4,795 training images. The strong view was configurable through crop, colour jitter, grayscale and Gaussian blur; separate one-component runs were used to determine whether one transformation was responsible for an apparent gain. This made it possible to test the semantic assignment itself, rather than merely changing the overall fraction of strongly augmented images.

The second July interface kept the same frozen concept scores and added a feature--concept dependence penalty to SimCLR. Since bird label and background are correlated in the training split, ResNet features $h_i=f_\theta(x_i)$ and concept weights were centred separately inside each target class. The penalty then averaged squared Pearson correlations over representation dimensions and selected concept coordinates:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{corr}}=\frac{1}{d|S|}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{k\in S}\operatorname{corr}(h_j,c_k\mid y)^2.
\end{equation}
The total loss was SimCLR plus $\lambda\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{corr}}$, with $\lambda\in\{0.001,0.01,0.1,1.0\}$. This branch therefore used target labels inside training; it was a controlled label-assisted baseline rather than a label-free method. Routing and Corr_Reg were evaluated with the same ResNet, optimizer, epoch budget and matched seeds. Periodic target probes, background probes, average accuracy and WGA were logged without selecting the final test result.

\subsection{SpLiCE Intervention Utility and edited targets}
SpLiCE-IU is group-free with respect to $a$ but target-label-assisted. An $\ell_1$ linear probe is cross-fitted on sparse codes. For held-out example $i$, deleting a candidate set $S$ changes the true-class probability by
\begin{equation}
    \delta_i(S)=p_{y_i}(c_i\text{ with }c_{iS}=0)-p_{y_i}(c_i).
\end{equation}
The utility is the class-balanced improvement on errors minus damage on correct predictions:
\begin{equation}
U(S)=\operatorname{CBMean}_{i:\hat y_i\neq y_i}[\delta_i(S)]_+
-\operatorname{CBMean}_{i:\hat y_i=y_i}[-\delta_i(S)]_+.
\end{equation}
Positive coordinates are greedily added while joint marginal utility remains positive. Selected coordinates are then zeroed or replaced by their within-target-class median. The edit preserves the full CLIP residual
\begin{equation}
\widetilde z_i=\operatorname{norm}\!\left(z_i+\alpha(c'_{iS}-c_{iS})D_S\right),
\end{equation}
and a separate stopped-gradient head distils $\widetilde z_i$ jointly with NT-Xent. This attempt tests explicit targets, but target labels remain necessary and the unexplained CLIP residual can retain the targeted information.

\subsection{SpLiCE-CRP v2: label-free projected relations}
\label{sec:crp}
CRP v1 proposed one hard neighbour selected after concept subtraction. It was demoted before full training because it lacked a defensible label-free factor selector, sparse subtraction could leave spurious information in the residual and a few rare donors could become hubs. CRP v2 addresses these issues with distributed concept groups, full-space projection, independent semantic agreement and soft relations.

\paragraph{Semantic factor groups.}
Active concepts are grouped using text-direction similarity, code coactivation and lexical deduplication. The full proposal additionally requires bootstrap stability, which is not yet part of the reported pilot audit. For a group $G$, near-collinear dictionary directions are removed and an orthonormal basis is formed:
\begin{equation}
    Q_G=\operatorname{orth}(D_G^\top).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Full-space intervention and relation gain.}
Let $u_i=\operatorname{norm}(z_i-\mu)$. Rather than subtracting only reconstructed coefficients, CRP removes the entire group subspace:
\begin{equation}
    u_i^{-G}=\operatorname{norm}\!\left((I-Q_GQ_G^\top)u_i\right).
\end{equation}
For candidate $j$, the intervention gain is
\begin{equation}
    \Delta(i,j,G)=\cos(u_i^{-G},u_j^{-G})-\cos(u_i,u_j).
\end{equation}
A relation is retained only if $\Delta>0$, group activations differ sufficiently, projected CLIP neighbours are reciprocal and the pair is supported by a frozen DINOv3 neighbourhood \citep{simeoni2025dinov3}. Fixed crops and bootstrap repeats are planned semantic-stability guards; the current frozen audit tests the simpler relation rule above.

\paragraph{Label-free selection and graph balancing.}
For each factor group, the implemented pilot score is
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{score}(G)=
\frac{\operatorname{gain}(G)\,\operatorname{coverage}(G)\,
\operatorname{agreement}(G)\,\operatorname{alignment}(G)}
{\operatorname{hubness}(G)}.
\end{equation}
Here alignment measures whether greater group-activation differences accompany greater intervention gains. The intended next version adds crop/bootstrap stability. Selection is calibrated against matched random subspaces and shuffled SpLiCE codes. Selecting no group is valid and yields SimCLR. Accepted edges form a top-$k$, row-stochastic teacher distribution $p_T(j\mid i)$ with indegree caps to prevent donor concentration.

\paragraph{Proposed relational transfer.}
For normalized student backbone features $h_i$, define $p_S(j\mid i)\propto\exp(\cos(h_i,h_j)/\tau_S)$ over the cached candidate set. The proposed objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{NT\text{-}Xent}}+\lambda(t)
\sum_i q_i\,\mathrm{KL}\!\left(p_T(\cdot\mid i)\,\|\,p_S(\cdot\mid i)\right),
\end{equation}
where $q_i$ is frozen graph confidence. A SimCLR-only warm-up and gradual $\lambda(t)$ ramp preserve the base objective. This loss is not yet implemented for the label-free graph because the frozen audit below returned \textsc{no-go}.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\paragraph{Data and evaluation.}
Completed experiments use Waterbirds \citep{sagawa2020groupdro}: 4,795 biased training images and four groups defined by bird type and background. A linear target probe is fitted on the designated probe-training split and evaluated on validation data; WGA is the minimum group accuracy. The test split remains unused while methods are selected. Legacy routing and regularization use 1,000 SSL epochs and paired seeds $1,3,4$. IU, CRP diagnostics and the relational oracle use a separately matched 500-epoch protocol; their absolute values must not be compared across budgets.

\paragraph{Controls.}
Routing is compared with shuffled per-image scores at the same routing rate. Edited CLIP distillation is compared with SimCLR, unedited CLIP targets, zero-out and matched random coordinates. CRP graph construction is compared with raw-CLIP kNN, DINO-only kNN, shuffled codes and matched random subspaces. Hidden labels are loaded only after graph creation to measure same-target precision and opposite-background coverage. An explicitly privileged relational oracle uses same-target, opposite-background pairs to test the downstream mechanism. Earlier reproducibility bugs were repaired so that periodic diagnostics no longer advance training RNG or change data order; only post-repair matched runs are aggregated.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Legacy interventions are not stable}
Table~\ref{tab:legacy} compresses the July sweep. The matched SimCLR reference reaches $48.04\pm2.82\%$ WGA over seeds $1,3,4$. Routing at $q=0.50$ improves two paired seeds by $+3.68$ and $+6.67$ points, then drops by $-11.58$ on the third seed; the failed run also has a final SSL loss of $0.109$ versus $0.026$ for its matched baseline. Increasing $q$ does not resolve this instability: the $q=0.95$ configuration reaches $46.93\pm6.10\%$, with paired changes of $-7.72,+7.46,-3.06$. The direct Top-5 routing control is more decisive. Semantic routing obtains $44.49\pm4.92\%$ WGA, shuffled routing obtains $46.40\pm3.24\%$, and the semantic-minus-shuffled changes are $-1.64,-0.32,-3.77$ on the three paired seeds. The same sign on every observed seed indicates that the per-image semantic assignment did not provide useful information beyond the routing rate.

The Corr_Reg sweep shows a different failure mode. At $\lambda=0.001$, the added term is approximately $7\times10^{-6}$ at the end of training and is effectively inactive. At $\lambda=0.1$, it reaches roughly $6\times10^{-4}$ and gives the best mean delta, only $+0.45$ points; its per-seed changes are $+2.38,+6.27,-7.30$. At $\lambda=1.0$, the term is about $4\times10^{-3}$ and the WGA mean falls to $43.84\%$. The penalty therefore has a narrow numerical scale: small values do not influence the representation, and larger values can remove useful class information together with the selected concept directions. Crop, colour-jitter, grayscale and blur component ablations each have only one comparable seed, so they do not establish a stable benefit. The background probe remains high, approximately $90.40$--$92.49\%$ on seed 1, which means these interventions did not show that background information had become inaccessible to a linear probe.

\subsection{Intervention utility finds task concepts}
On the frozen SpLiCE-CBM diagnostic (Table~\ref{tab:discovery}), IU selects \texttt{birds}, \texttt{heron} and \texttt{crow}. Its $+0.75$ WGA change is weak and reduces average accuracy. Error contrast and the gradient-probe selector are strongly harmful. In contrast, the group-aware oracle selects background-like concepts and improves WGA by $9.02$ points. The gap separates signal availability in SpLiCE from effective feature selection.

\subsection{The current CRP graph fails the frozen gate}
Across three null-calibration seeds, CRP audits 139 groups and selects 116--121. The result is stable but not useful: median Jaccard@20 between raw and projected neighbours is $0.9647$, median top-1 turnover is only $0.0229$ and the audit returns \textsc{no-go}. The resulting graph covers $85.42\%$ of anchors and has high same-target precision ($94.65\%$), but only $0.96\%$ of its same-target edges cross backgrounds. Raw CLIP kNN has almost the same target precision ($94.46\%$) and a substantially higher $2.81\%$ cross-background rate; DINO kNN gives $92.48\%$ and $1.09\%$, respectively. CRP therefore mainly reproduces existing neighbourhoods, yielding little additional cross-background structure.

The privileged relational oracle is informative in the opposite direction. At the same 500-epoch budget and seed, correct same-target/opposite-background relations improve WGA from $40.66\%$ to $45.39\%$. One seed is not evidence of a robust method, but it separates two failure hypotheses, pointing to relational signal quality as the remaining limitation of the label-free frozen graph.

\section{Discussion: Novelty, Evidence and Next Decision}
\paragraph{What is new.}
Existing concept mitigation methods mainly edit the representation in which concepts were discovered or rebalance a supervised classifier. Existing robust SSL methods modify sampling, augmentation, or global geometry without an interpretable intervention. CRP's intended novelty is their connection: language-aligned concept groups define full-space counterfactual projections; an independent SSL geometry filters unsafe edits; and the accepted \emph{relations}, rather than edited CLIP vectors or one hard image, supervise a separately trained backbone. The shuffled-code, random-subspace, raw-CLIP and DINO-only controls distinguish the value of concept-conditioned intervention from ordinary teacher distillation.

\paragraph{What is established.}
The completed results support four claims. Privileged group information identifies background-related coordinates in SpLiCE that support a substantially stronger intervention. Scalar routing and coordinate-wise correlation removal don't transfer that information reliably. Target-error-based discovery preferentially finds target concepts, providing weak guidance for feature removal. Finally, correct relational supervision can improve a one-seed pilot, but the current label-free CRP projection changes too few neighbours and discovers fewer cross-background relations than raw CLIP.

\paragraph{What remains hypothetical.}
There is no evidence yet that CRP v2 improves WGA, that DINO consensus reliably preserves task identity, or that the label-free score generalizes beyond Waterbirds. The next experiment should therefore improve or reject the frozen mechanism. Alternatives are: stronger but controlled interventions (forbidden-group sparse refitting or projection of better distributed concept groups), stricter group selection against nulls and direct comparison with raw-CLIP and DINO graph turnover.

\section{Limitations}
All completed results are on Waterbirds. Legacy multi-seed results use only three seeds, while the IU and relational pilots use one. Validation probes are reported because the test split is protected during method development. The 500- and 1,000-epoch protocols are internally matched but not cross-comparable. Hidden annotations are used in post-hoc graph analysis and explicitly privileged oracles; they must not influence CRP thresholds.

 Generally, feature relevance is seen as task-dependent. CRP assumes that removing the selected factor restores relations supported by an independent SSL model, but CLIP and DINO may share the same shortcut. Concept groups may entangle class and contextual information, so full projection can remove both. Sparse Waterbirds minorities can still cause low counterfactual coverage despite graph balancing. Finally, the exact equality of two pairs of seed-0 editing controls indicates degenerate edits that require target-displacement diagnostics before further training.

\section{Conclusion}
This study narrows the search for spurious-robust SSL from concept availability to intervention and relation discovery. Privileged selection reveals actionable structure in SpLiCE, motivating a search for transfer mechanisms beyond the tested routing, regularization, and sparse-editing interfaces. SpLiCE-CRP v2 reframes this search as concept-projected relation discovery. Its current frozen audit localizes the remaining bottleneck to a projection that barely changes local geometry and yields fewer cross-background relations than raw CLIP.
